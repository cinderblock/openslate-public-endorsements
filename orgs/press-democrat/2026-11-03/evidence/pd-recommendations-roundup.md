Here are the PD Editorial Board recommendations for the Nov. 3 election.
Federal
House of Representatives, District 4 — Mike Thompson
There’s a lot of pushback right now against so-called lifers in Washington, D.C. Eric Jones, who is taking on incumbent Mike Thompson is one calling out those lifers. The Press Democrat agrees with Jones and his desire to hold politicians accountable. We also recognize the need for change in Washington, but even more so we appreciate strength in stability. The Press Democrat recommends a yes vote for Mike Thompson for California’s 4th Congressional District. We think Jones has great ideas, but we recommend he gain experience at the municipal or county level first; his communities would greatly benefit. The Press Democrat endorses Mike Thompson for California’s 4th Congressional District.

House of Representatives, 1st District — Mike McGuire
Mike McGuire is a lifelong North Coast resident and brings deep experience at every level of government, having served as a state senator, a Sonoma County supervisor, a Healdsburg city councilman and, beginning at age 19, a Healdsburg school board member. McGuire is one of California’s most effective legislators, the first rural representative to lead the upper house as president pro tem in a half-century, and after disasters in Sonoma, Mendocino and Lake counties, he is authority on wildfire prevention and recovery. He’s ready to represent the North Coast in Congress.

State measures
Prop 3: High-income tax — Yes
This measure would make permanent higher income tax rates that voters first approved in 2012 to support education and extended in 2016. Those rates would otherwise expire in 2031. The higher rates apply only to income above $371,000 for single filers and $742,000 for joint filers. Those are 2025 thresholds and are adjusted annually for inflation. Letting them expire would amount to a huge tax cut for high earners in 2031 that would cost the state up to $15 billion annually, much of it for K-12 schools and community colleges. The Press Democrat recommends a yes vote.
To read our complete editorial, click here.
Prop 4: Public fundraising — No
A yes vote on California Prop. 4 would allow public officers and candidates to accept public funds — taxpayer dollars — for campaign purposes. A yes vote would allow state and local governments to create programs so that public funds — again, taxpayer dollars — could be set aside for election campaigns. The Press Democrat recommends voting no on Prop. 4. Our tax dollars should not be spent on election campaigns.
To read our complete editorial, click here.
Prop 40: Billionaire tax — Yes
This measure proposes imposing a one-time 5% tax on the net worth of about 200 billionaires who lived in the state on Jan. 1, 2026. They could pay that in installments over five years. It isn’t a permanent fix to the state’s budget issues, but the cash infusion could pay for doctors, nurses, hospitals and clinics that serve people who cannot afford health care otherwise. The Press Democrat recommends a yes vote on Prop. 40.
To read our complete editorial, click here.
Prop. 41: Tax audits — No
The measure claims to be an accountability measure that would require better auditing of new special taxes and compliance with the state’s voter-approved spending limit. California has plenty of cases of spending racing out of control without much accountability, but Prop. 41 uses that as a diversion. The Press Democrat recommends a no vote on Prop. 41.
To read our complete editorial, click here.
Prop. 42: Property taxes — No
The proposed measure would ban any new state tax on personal property enacted after Jan. 1, 2026. Taxes like, say, Prop. 40’s billionaire tax. It would also prohibit taxes that apply retroactively. The more troubling part of the measure is that it would take an entire class of taxes off the table for good. The Press Democrat recommends a no vote on Prop. 42.
To read our complete editorial, click here.
Prop. 43: Tax threshold — No
Proposition 43 would amend the state constitution to require two-thirds approval for special taxes proposed by residents, the requirement that special taxes proposed by local governments now face. If voters approve it, that change would apply to measures passed after Jan. 1, 2027. It aims to tackle what supporters call a “loophole” in the rules for enacting local tax measures. The system, admittedly, is strange, but it works. We recommend Californians vote no on Prop. 43.
To read our complete editorial, click here.
Prop. 44: Clinic funding — No
Proposition 44 would require community clinics to spend at least 90% of their revenue on patient care and the services that support it, and to report publicly how their money is spent. No more than 10% can go to executive pay, administration and overhead. But the measure would set strict spending requirements for nonprofit health clinics that could put precious revenue at risk. The Press Democrat recommends a no vote on Prop. 44.

State candidates
Lieutenant governor — Fiona Ma
Among the two candidates left on the Nov. 3 ballot — Fiona Ma, a Democrat, and Gloria Romero, a Republican — who could lead this state’s 40 million people and oversee its $4 trillion economy? The only answer is Ma. What convinces us that Ma can actually move the needle on her agenda items is the fact that she has already been knee-deep advancing them in a senior executive capacity. The Press Democrat endorses Fiona Ma.
To read our complete editorial, click here.
State treasurer — Eleni Kounalakis
California’s next treasurer should expect to inherit the most financially volatile environment since the Great Recession. Of the two candidates on Californians’ Nov. 3 ballots — Eleni Kounalakis, a Democrat, and Jennifer Hawks, a Republican — we support the one whose résumé instills confidence, not the one who has repeatedly declined to share her résumé. That is Kounalakis. The Press Democrat endorses Elena Kounalakis.
To read our complete editorial, click here.
Insurance commissioner — Ben Allen
With the Nov. 3 election approaching, California voters must understand: When they choose between the two Democrats — Ben Allen and Jane Kim — running to replace termed-out Insurance Commissioner Ricardo Lara, the viability of homeownership is on the ballot. Ben Allen is our choice to navigate California through this unprecedented crisis. Doing this job right requires navigating state politics, negotiating with competing economic interest groups and managing a 1,400-person bureaucracy that oversees more than 1,600 insurance companies. Ben Allen convinced us he could operate inside this maze. His opponent, Jane Kim, did not. The Press Democrat endorses Ben Allen.

Superintendent of public instruction — Richard Barrera
The state’s next superintendent of public instruction will need not only to confront a struggling school system, but also to restructure the office to ensure it continues to be relevant, meaningful and useful to taxpayers. Of the two candidates on the Nov. 3 ballot, Richard Barrera is best suited to these ends.

State Senate, 2nd District — Damon Connolly
Damon Connolly has lifelong ties to the North Bay and deep experience in state and local government. He worked as a deputy state attorney general and served on a school board, a city council and the Marin County Board of Supervisors before being elected to the state Legislature in 2022. A two-term member of the state Assembly, he has passed laws requiring schools in fire-prone areas to prepare evacuation plans and tightening conflict-of-interest regulations for mobile home inspections. He is presently pushing legislation that would eliminate a pathway used by multinational corporations to offshore profits and avoid paying California taxes. He is running to succeed Mike McGuire.
To read our complete editorial, click here.
Assembly, 12th District — Eric Lucan
Eric Lucan, who is running to succeed Damon Connolly, offers a record of public service and private sector experience. He was head of marketing for Mike’s Bikes and served on Novato’s parks and recreation commission and City Council before being elected to the Board of Supervisors in 2022. He was appointed to the SMART board in 2012 and, except for one two-year gap, has served there since. Lucan’s years on the SMART board position him well to assist in ongoing expansion of the rail system and its further integration with North Bay public transit agencies. He also is committed to upgrading Highway 37, both to ease traffic congestion and to protect this vital transportation corridor from inundation as a result of rising sea levels.

Sonoma County measures
Measure H: Renewal of Sonoma County Regional Park sales tax — Yes
The Sonoma County’s regional parks sales tax delivers a lot for a little. The one-eighth cent sales tax — that’s about 3 cents for every $25 purchase — was approved by nearly 73% of voters in November 2018. In the five-year time span, park-goers have seen improvements in city and county parks alike. The Press Democrat supported the regional parks sales tax in 2018 and we recommend a yes vote on the Nov. 3 ballot.
To read our complete editorial, click here.
Measure AB: Santa Rosa Junior College bond  — Yes
For the third time in 24 years, leaders at Santa Rosa Junior College are turning to the public for financial assistance. The price tag is a sticker shock, but it’s important to remember the value that SRJC brings to Sonoma County and beyond. Students who attend the junior college gain about $7.20 in lifetime earnings for every dollar they spend on their education. And taxpayers receive about $1.40 back in direct benefits. If Measure AB is approved, the cost, spread out over the entire county to local homeowners, would continue to be $30 per $100,000 of assessed value of local property. The Press Democrat believes that’s a fair price to continue to pay to extend the value the SRJC provides to the community. We recommend a yes vote on Measure AB.
To read our complete editorial, click here.
Measure I: Windsor sales tax — Yes
Windsor sales tax (Measure I): Measure I, Windsor’s proposed one-cent sales tax, has been a long time coming. For its 33-year history, the town of Windsor has operated on a mostly self-sustaining budget, making it through some of the toughest economic times in modern history, including the 2008 Recession, with fiscal conservancy and acuity. The Press Democrat recommends a yes vote on Measure I.

Measure K: Rohnert Park sales tax — Yes
After years of filling budget deficits with general fund reserves, the Rohnert Park City Council has turned to voters for help. The measure requires a simple majority to pass. If it moves forward, Measure K would bring an expected $5.5 million annually into city coffers. The Press Democrat recommends voters maintain current service and staffing levels by voting yes for Measure K.
To read our complete editorial, click here.
Measure L: Santa Rosa sales tax — Yes
Santa Rosa leaders have painted a pretty bleak picture. After massive cuts to their staffing and departments for three consecutive years, eliminating 82 positions, consolidating departments and restructuring pension liability, expenses for running the city continue to outstrip revenue. Leaders now say if the city cannot bring in more revenue, both the city’s police and fire departments — which comprise roughly two-thirds of the city’s general fund budget — would face more cuts — this time more brutal. The Press Democrat recommends a yes vote on Measure L.

Sonoma County candidates
Supervisor, 2nd District — Sylvia Lemus
Sylvia Lemus has the deepest résumé in the four candidate field to succeed Supervisor David Rabbitt. She worked for the county for three decades, was a member on the task force that recommended creation of the county’s law enforcement oversight agency and has served on both the planning commission and City Council in Cotati. Beyond the workplace, Lemus was a founder of Los Cien, a local business and social justice organization, and the Cinco de Mayo Festival in Roseland.

Supervisor, 4th District — Tom Schwedhelm
Tom Schwedhelm spent three decades in law enforcement, starting as a correctional officer in the Sonoma County Jail. From there, he joined the Santa Rosa Police Department, retiring as chief in 2013. He was elected to the City Council the following year, serving two terms and presiding as mayor in 2018 and 2019. Schwedhelm’s well-rounded experience and knowledge are matched by broad support, including endorsements from four incumbent supervisors and numerous elected officials across the county. He is the right pick to succeed James Gore.

Santa Rosa City Council, District 4 — Melanie Jones-Carter
Residents in Santa Rosa’s District 4, which encompasses McDonald Avenue, Junior College, Hidden Valley and Fountaingrove neighborhoods in the northeast part of the city, have a tough decision to make this November. The Press Democrat recommends a yes vote for Melanie Jones-Carter, a longtime community organizer and chair of the city’s Design Review and Preservation Board. Jones-Carter’s experience and organizational management will be assets to a Santa Rosa City Council struggling with a budget deficit and leadership turnover.

Healdsburg City Council, District 2 — Mathew López
The race has been whittled down to Scott Pratt and Mathew López. Pratt has lived in Healdsburg since 2010, moving to the city from southern California. If elected, Pratt has many items to tackle on his to-do list, from developing “appropriate affordable low- and middle-income housing alternatives,” and digging “deeper into the use of Flock camera data,” to expanding water security, climate protection and climate change preparedness. López, a bilingual speaker, is running on a campaign of sustainable housing, smart city planning, economic development and improved transit. While he might be a green politician — he has some civic experience serving as vice chair on the city’s SMART Station Area Committees — his fresh ideas and youthful attitude are exactly what the Healdsburg City Council needs. The Press Democrat recommends Mathew López.

Petaluma City Council — Janice Cader Thompson (District 1), Brent Newell (District 2), Karen Nau (District 3) and Kevin McDonnell (Mayor)
The Press Democrat endorses incumbent Janice Cader Thompson for District 1, newcomer Brent Newell for District 2, incumbent Karen Nau for District 3 and incumbent Kevin McDonnell for mayor. While Cader Thompson can — at times — be a loose cannon at the dais, there’s no doubt that she is a fighter for her constituents. We like Newell’s fresh attitude, and his efforts and push to meet as many community members as possible have not gone unnoticed, as he has connected with groups throughout the district. Nau’s experience, kindness and keen ability to serve as a connection between the city’s different factions is vital for Petaluma residents right now. McDonnell’s leadership and expertise managing budget crises, as well as his ability to speak to all Petalumans, will be a boon as the city grapples with that uncertainty.

Rohnert Park City Council — Linda Khoury Tams (District 2) and Susan Hollingsworth Adams (District 5)
Voters in Districts 2 and 5 will elect council members this year. A new council member will serve in District 2, as current Mayor Emily Sanborn chose not to seek re-election. The Press Democrat recommends Linda Khoury Tams. The council and the city’s residents would benefit from a representative with a strong civic understanding and who could serve as a liaison to the Rohnert Park business community. In District 5, The Press Democrat recommends incumbent Susan Hollingsworth Adams. The two-time council member has been a fiscal stalwart, thinking critically about every dollar the council has to spend.

Cloverdale City Council — Andrés Marquez, Chris Parker and Will Carter
It’s no surprise to Cloverdale residents that its city council is a bit dysfunctional. That kind of unchecked leadership needs balances and pushback, especially as power has grown, become more bifurcated and made the council’s work less transparent to the public and media alike. The Press Democrat believes that incumbent Andrés Marquez, former Police Chief Chris Parker and political newcomer Will Carter can provide those checks and balances and enable the council to once again serve the city’s constituents. We recommend a vote for Marquez, Parker and Carter.

Sonoma City Council, District 1 — Julian Mackie
Three candidates are vying for this new seat in the Nov. 3 election: Julian Mackie, a nonprofit executive director, Peggy Hutton, a small business owner, and Michael Menefee, a retired firefighter-paramedic. Because of Julian Mackie’s big-picture, beyond-the-usual ideas about the city of Sonoma and its future, The Press Democrat is endorsing him for Sonoma City Council District 1. Although he is not from Sonoma — a trait that some might consider blasphemous — his passion is pure and his understanding of the community comes from a deep love for the place he now calls home.

Napa County
Measure B: Wildfire and open space sales tax — Yes
Wildfire resilience and conservation cannot be achieved in isolation. Protecting watersheds, managing large parks, restoring overgrown open spaces and maintaining buffer zones around towns are directly linked to reducing fire risks for adjacent communities. Such work is vital to the continued protection of Napa’s residents and lands from the next big fire. The Press Democrat recommends a yes vote for Napa County’s Measure B.
To read our complete editorial, click here.
Measure P: American Canyon sales tax — No
American Canyon sales tax (Measure P): American Canyon leadership has lofty goals. At the same time, the city council is also asking voters this November to approve Measure P, a new one-cent sales tax measure. Measure P would raise about $3.7 million annually for the city by raising the local sales tax rate from 7.75% to 8.75%. The sales tax, which does not have a sunset date, needs a simple majority to pass. City Manager Jason Holley says the revenue would help fund general city services, including a wide array of parks and recreation projects identified under a new plan. Right now, because residents cannot look at city operations and say they’re lean and mean, the Press Democrat recommends a No vote on Measure P.
