## About This Guide
This is a pragmatic, nonpartisan voter guide designed to help Los Angeles voters identify and vote against extremist candidates backed by ideological special interests, including the Democratic Socialist of America (DSA). Our recommendations are based on candidates’ records, affiliations, endorsements, and approach to governing, not their political party.
Los Angeles County is overwhelmingly Democratic, so many of the candidates who appear in this guide, including candidates we recommend, are Democrats. The distinction we draw is not Democrat versus Republican. It is between pragmatic candidates focused on effective governance and candidates aligned with ideological extremes.
If an office or race does not appear in this guide, it means we have not identified a DSA-backed or affiliated candidate in that race. In those contests, voters should review the candidates and make their own decision.

## LA Mayor1 endorsement
LOS ANGELES CITY GENERAL MUNICIPAL ELECTION Mayor
KAREN RUTH BASS▾
What does this position do?▾
The mayor runs the city. That means setting budget priorities for a $13 billion operation, appointing the heads of departments like the LAPD and the Department of Building and Safety, and driving the agenda on everything from homelessness to permitting timelines. When a pothole doesn't get fixed, when a new apartment building takes five years to approve, when 911 response times slip, the buck stops at the mayor's desk.
The stakes here are enormous. The mayor decides whether Los Angeles prioritizes public safety or treats policing as optional. The mayor controls whether building permits move fast enough to actually get housing built, or whether red tape keeps choking supply and driving rents higher. Every new city program the mayor proposes has to be paid for, and that means either cutting something else or reaching deeper into your wallet through taxes and fees. Watch for candidates who talk about "new revenue" without naming cuts. That is code for raising your costs. This office shapes whether City Hall respects working families as taxpayers and customers, or treats them as a piggy bank for expanding bureaucracy.
Why we endorse
Karen Bass is the 43rd Mayor of Los Angeles, the first woman and second African American to hold the office. Before entering politics, she worked as a physician assistant and founded Community Coalition in South LA. She served in the U.S. House of Representatives from 2011 to 2022 and made history as Speaker of the California State Assembly, the first Black woman to lead a state legislative body in U.S. history.
This is a practical endorsement, not an ideological one. Bass has real deficiencies on housing production and fiscal discipline. She opposed SB 79, which would have unlocked dense housing near transit. She reversed fast-tracking for affordable housing in single-family neighborhoods, putting an estimated 1,443 potential units in limbo. She supported Measure A, a sales tax increase that burdens every household in the city. These are serious failures. But the alternative is dramatically worse. Her opponent, Nithya Raman, is a DSA-endorsed activist who has voted against banning encampments near schools, opposed enforcement-based approaches to homelessness, and has aligned herself with the most anti-housing-provider factions on the City Council. A Raman mayoralty would mean an ideological agenda imposed citywide: more restrictions on housing providers, more hostility toward the businesses that create jobs, and a retreat from the public safety gains Los Angeles has made. Raman's council record includes votes that undermine LAPD operations and embolden the encampment crisis. Replacing Bass with Raman would not fix what Bass has gotten wrong. It would make every problem worse.
On public safety, Bass has delivered measurable results. Homicides and violent crime have declined during her tenure. LAPD applicant numbers have reached record highs. She declared a state of emergency on homelessness as her first act in office and reports that over 21,000 homeless individuals were moved indoors in her first year. She has supported enforcement tools including no-camping zones, a direct contrast with Raman's opposition to those same tools. Bass carries the endorsement of the Los Angeles Police Protective League (LAPPL), LA County Sheriff Robert Luna, and every LA County Supervisor. She also signed an executive order in April 2026 aimed at streamlining residential construction through reforms like self-certification, a step in the right direction even if it does not go far enough.
Karen Bass is the better choice for a city that cannot afford to hand its top office to ideological experimentation.
Key positions
LAPPL-Endorsed on Public Safety: Bass has the endorsement of the Los Angeles Police Protective League and LA County Sheriff Robert Luna. Homicides and violent crime have declined during her tenure, and LAPD applicant numbers have reached record highs. Her opponent voted against banning encampments near schools and has consistently opposed enforcement tools.
Homelessness Through Enforcement: Bass declared a city state of emergency on homelessness on her first day in office and reports over 21,000 people moved indoors in her first year. She supports no-camping zones and shelter-first enforcement, a direct contrast with her DSA-endorsed opponent's services-only approach that has failed to reduce street homelessness.
Construction Streamlining Executive Order: Her April 2026 executive order introduced self-certification and other reforms to speed residential construction permitting. While her broader housing record is mixed, these reforms move in the right direction and would be reversed under a mayor aligned with anti-development activist organizations.
Broad Business Community Support: Bass is endorsed by the Central City Association PAC, BizFed PAC, the Hollywood Chamber of Commerce PAC, Valley Industry and Commerce Association (VICA) PAC, and the LA Area Chamber of Commerce's L.A. Jobs PAC. This broad business alignment signals a mayor who will not wage war on the employers and investors Los Angeles depends on.
Her Opponent Would Be Worse: Nithya Raman is DSA-endorsed, has aligned with Ground Game LA and the most anti-housing-provider members of the City Council, and has voted to obstruct LAPD operations and encampment enforcement. A Raman mayoralty would mean more regulation, more tax burden, and less public safety at a time the city can afford none of it.
## LA City Council2 endorsements
What does this position do?▾
Your City Council member is the single most powerful elected official in your daily life. Each of the 15 council members controls land use decisions in their district, meaning they can speed up or block new housing, approve or kill a small business permit, and decide whether a homeless encampment on your block gets addressed or ignored. They vote on your property tax rate, set rules for housing providers (including the family renting out their duplex), and shape how the LAPD and fire department are funded and deployed. The City Council also controls a $13 billion annual budget with very little outside oversight.
Here is what is at stake. Council members regularly push new taxes, new fees on development, and new regulations on rental housing that sound compassionate but shrink the supply of homes and raise costs for everyone. Every dollar the city spends comes from somewhere: your rent, your grocery bill, the price of hiring a plumber. Watch whether your council member treats housing like a market that needs more supply or a system that needs more rules. Watch whether they prioritize public safety funding or redirect it. Watch whether they streamline permits so someone can actually open a taco shop in six weeks instead of six months. This office touches your rent, your safety, your commute, and your cost of living more than almost any seat in Sacramento or Washington.

District 3 needs a councilmember who understands what it costs to meet a payroll, sign a lease, and navigate the city's permitting bureaucracy. If elected, Gaspar would be the only member of the Los Angeles City Council who has ever built and run a business. His opponent, Barri Worth Girvan, carries the endorsement of DSA-LA, an organization whose agenda includes expanded rent control, new taxes on housing providers, and a "care-first" approach to homelessness that rejects enforcement. The West Valley cannot afford a councilmember beholden to that coalition. Gaspar offers the opposite: a pro-growth, enforcement-first agenda backed by the people who keep the city safe and the economy running.
Gaspar's endorsement sheet reads like a blueprint for competent city governance. He is the only candidate in the race endorsed by the Los Angeles Police Protective League. He also carries the Association for Los Angeles Deputy Sheriffs, the LA Airport Peace Officers Association, and the LA County Professional Peace Officers' Association. On the business side, BizFed, the LA Chamber PAC, the Valley Industry and Commerce Association, the Apartment Association of Greater Los Angeles, and the Southland Regional Association of Realtors all back his campaign. Former Councilmember Laura Chick, outgoing CD3 Councilmember Bob Blumenfield, Councilmembers John Lee and Monica Rodriguez, LA County Supervisor Kathryn Barger, and third-place primary finisher Christopher Robert Celona have all endorsed him.
On homelessness, Gaspar has done the math that City Hall refuses to do. He points out the city spends over $700 million a year on homelessness with dismal results: Inside Safe costs $257,000 per person for permanent housing placements and $80,000 annually per motel room. His alternative is zero tolerance for illegal encampments, aggressive enforcement of anti-camping laws, and partnerships with cost-effective organizations like the Salvation Army. He has already coordinated encampment clearances with LAPD through his "Serving Before Swearing In" initiative, delivering results before taking the oath of office.
On housing, Gaspar supports cutting the red tape that drives up construction costs and slows building timelines. He backs development in appropriate areas like Warner Center while opposing mandated density increases in single-family neighborhoods. He opposes rent control expansions that discourage new housing investment. On the economy, he wants a top-to-bottom review of the city's permitting and licensing process, a crackdown on unlicensed operators who undercut legitimate businesses, and the restoration of a standalone economic development department.
Tim Gaspar is the pro-growth, pro-safety, pro-accountability leader District 3 has been waiting for.

Law Enforcement's Clear Choice: Gaspar is the only candidate in the CD3 race endorsed by the LAPPL. He also carries ALADS, LAAPOA, and the LA County Professional Peace Officers' Association. He supports hiring and retaining more LAPD officers and firefighters and has criticized the city for letting staffing fall to early-1990s levels.
Zero Tolerance on Encampments: Gaspar calls the city's $700 million annual homelessness spend a failure, citing Inside Safe costs of $257,000 per permanent placement. He enforces anti-camping laws, has already coordinated clearances with LAPD before taking office, and partners with cost-effective providers like the Salvation Army.
Pro-Housing, Anti-Red-Tape: Endorsed by the Apartment Association of Greater Los Angeles and the Southland Regional Association of Realtors, Gaspar opposes rent control expansions and supports streamlining permits to bring housing costs down. He backs smart development in Warner Center while protecting single-family neighborhoods from forced density mandates.
Business Builder, Not Bureaucrat: Gaspar built a 60-person, 11,000-client insurance firm from scratch. He would be the only councilmember who has ever run a business. He is endorsed by BizFed, the LA Chamber PAC, VICA, and Rick Caruso, and advocates eliminating permitting delays that drive businesses out of the city.
DSA-Endorsed Opponent: Gaspar's runoff opponent carries the endorsement of DSA-LA, which supports expanded rent control, new taxes on housing providers, and services-only homelessness policies that reject enforcement. District 3 voters have a clear choice between an activist agenda and accountable, results-driven leadership.

Jose Ugarte is the results-oriented leader District 9 needs.

## Community College Board3 endorsements
What does this position do?▾
The community college trustee sets the budget, hires the chancellor, and approves programs for the nine campuses in the Los Angeles Community College District. That means this board directly shapes whether a working adult can afford to retrain for a better job, whether a recent high school grad has a real path to a four-year degree, and whether your local campus actually feels safe to walk through at night.
What most people miss is that this board controls over $6 billion in bond money and a massive operating budget funded by your property taxes. Trustees decide how efficiently those dollars get spent. Do they go toward classroom instruction and workforce programs that lead to actual jobs, or do they get absorbed by administrative bloat and pet projects? Every dollar wasted here is a dollar taken from a taxpayer who never gets it back. Voters should pay attention to whether candidates prioritize fiscal discipline, campus safety, and programs that connect students to real economic opportunity rather than expanding bureaucracy.

## Other LA City Offices1 endorsement
LOS ANGELES CITY GENERAL MUNICIPAL ELECTION City Attorney
JOHN MCKINNEY▾
What does this position do?▾
The City Attorney is Los Angeles's top lawyer, and the decisions made in that office touch your life more than you probably realize. This is the person who decides which lawsuits to file on behalf of the city, defends L.A. when it gets sued, drafts the legal language behind every new rule the City Council passes, and prosecutes misdemeanors like vandalism, illegal dumping, and quality-of-life crimes in your neighborhood. When the city loses a lawsuit, you pay the settlement out of your tax dollars. L.A. has hemorrhaged hundreds of millions in legal payouts in recent years.
Here is what to watch for. A City Attorney shapes whether L.A. actually enforces public safety laws or lets them collect dust. This office also determines how aggressively the city defends regulations that drive up housing costs or restrict what small property owners can do with their buildings. Every new mandate the Council dreams up needs the City Attorney's sign-off, which means this seat is a quiet but powerful checkpoint on whether city policy helps people find affordable housing or just adds red tape that shrinks the supply. Look for someone who treats taxpayer liability like real money, prioritizes public safety prosecution, and understands that legal overreach has real costs for renters, businesses, and working families.
Why we endorse
John McKinney is a Deputy District Attorney with nearly 30 years of prosecutorial experience in the Los Angeles County District Attorney's Office, where he currently serves as Director of Specialized Prosecutions. In that role, he oversees eight divisions covering juveniles, family violence, sex crimes, narcotics, major crimes, and gang homicide. He earned his J.D. from UCLA School of Law in 1997 and has tried over 100 jury trials, including nearly 40 murder cases with a reported 97% conviction rate.
The City Attorney's office is the legal backbone of Los Angeles. It drafts the laws the Council passes, defends the city in court, and prosecutes misdemeanor crimes that drive residents out of their neighborhoods. Since 2020, the city has paid out nearly $1.1 billion in liability settlements and judgments. That is not a legal department performing at the level this city demands. McKinney would bring a prosecutor's discipline to an office that desperately needs it: aggressive risk management to reduce costly payouts, early settlement of meritorious claims, vigorous defense against frivolous ones, and far less outsourcing of legal work to expensive private law firms.
McKinney's public safety credentials are unmatched in this race. He has spent his entire career in prosecution and law enforcement partnership, and his priorities reflect the quality-of-life issues Angelenos raise at every town hall: repeat offenders, organized retail theft, street racing, open-air drug markets, and illegal firearms. On homelessness, he supports an enforcement-based approach that keeps sidewalks passable, protects parks and schools, and addresses dangerous encampments, combining accountability with diversion programs rather than a permissive, services-only model. His endorsement from the Los Angeles Police Protective League is the clearest signal that the officers who do the work trust him to back them up. He also carries endorsements from District Attorney Nathan Hochman, former District Attorneys Steve Cooley and Jackie Lacey, Councilmembers Traci Park, Tim McOsker, John Lee, and Monica Rodriguez, the LA Daily News editorial board, and the Apartment Association of Greater Los Angeles.
John McKinney is the experienced, results-driven City Attorney Los Angeles needs.
Key positions
Proven Prosecutor, Day One: Nearly 30 years in the DA's office. Over 100 jury trials, including nearly 40 murder cases with a 97% conviction rate. He does not need on-the-job training.
Backed by Law Enforcement: Endorsed by the Los Angeles Police Protective League, DA Nathan Hochman, and former DAs Steve Cooley and Jackie Lacey. The people closest to public safety trust McKinney to lead this office.
Fiscal Accountability in Legal Spending: The city has paid out nearly $1.1 billion in liability costs since 2020. McKinney has pledged aggressive risk management, early settlement of meritorious cases, and reduced outsourcing to expensive private law firms to stop the hemorrhaging.
Enforcement on Quality-of-Life Crimes: Prioritizes prosecuting repeat offenders, organized shoplifting, street racing, open-air drug markets, and illegal firearms possession. These are the crimes that hollow out neighborhoods and drive businesses away.
Compassionate Accountability on Homelessness: Supports keeping sidewalks passable, protecting parks and schools, and clearing dangerous encampments. Combines enforcement with service access rather than adopting a permissive, services-only model.
## State Senate2 endorsements
What does this position do?▾
Your state senator sits in Sacramento and votes on the laws that shape your daily costs, your commute, and whether enough housing gets built in your neighborhood. The 40-member State Senate approves the California budget, sets tax rates, writes the rules for housing construction and rent regulation, and confirms appointments to powerful boards that oversee everything from utility rates to parole decisions. PROPERTY TRANSFER TAX EXEMPTION FOR VICTIMS OF JANUARY 2025 FIRE DISASTER. PROPOSITION TE.▾
What it does
Shall an ordinance be adopted to provide a one-time exemption from the City's special documentary transfer tax (Proposition ULA) for residential properties that were damaged or destroyed in the Palisades Fire and are sold within five years of the date of the Fire?
What does this measure propose?▾
If your home was damaged or destroyed in the January 2025 fires, selling it today means paying the city's special transfer tax on top of everything else you've already lost. That tax, created by Measure ULA, adds a steep surcharge on property sales above a certain price. Proposition TE would give fire victims a one-time pass on that extra tax when they sell their damaged residential property, as long as the sale happens within five years of the disaster.
Here is what is at stake. The transfer tax was bad policy from the start because it punishes property sales and discourages housing investment. TE acknowledges that reality for one narrow group of people in one specific disaster. It does not repeal the tax or help anyone else burdened by it. The cost falls on no one, because it simply stops the city from collecting money it should never have been charging. If you think fire victims deserve relief, the question is whether a five-year, fire-only carveout goes far enough, or whether the deeper problem is a transfer tax that hurts every seller, every buyer, and every future renter citywide.
Why we support
Measure TE would grant a one-time exemption from the Proposition ULA transfer tax for residential properties damaged or destroyed in the January 2025 Palisades Fire. The exemption applies to original owners as of January 7, 2025, covers one transaction per property, and expires five years after the fire. It also authorizes refunds for any ULA tax already paid on qualifying sales before the measure takes effect.
The case for Measure TE is simple: fire victims should not be punished for selling what remains of their homes. Proposition ULA imposes a 4% to 5.5% surcharge on higher-value property transactions. In Pacific Palisades, where median home values far exceed ULA's threshold, that surcharge can add hundreds of thousands of dollars to the cost of selling. Families who lost everything in January 2025 should not face a six-figure tax bill for choosing to move on rather than rebuild. That is not a policy choice. It is cruelty by spreadsheet.
Measure TE also serves as an inadvertent confession. The City Council is acknowledging what economists and housing providers have said since Measure ULA passed: the tax discourages property transactions.
