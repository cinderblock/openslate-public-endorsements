Local Ballot Measures
Measure A (NO)
‍ City and County of San Francisco Commission Shuffle and Pension Board Giveaway
Sold as streamlining while eliminating just three of the city's hundred-plus commissions, it makes pension board members nearly impossible to remove, hands organized labor a guaranteed seat on the commission that sets water and power rates, strips the city's authority to discipline striking government employees, and lets politicians abolish the watchdog committee voters created to guard homelessness tax dollars.
Measure B (NO)
Creating a City and County San Francisco Government-Run Bank
Puts taxpayers on the hook for a loan portfolio written by political appointees, where every lending decision becomes a political decision, every loss lands on the general fund, and the whole operation sits outside the budget scrutiny that applies to real city departments.
Measure C (NO)
‍ San Francisco Housing Trust Fund Budget Lock-Up
Locks up an ever-growing share of the city's general fund for another thirty-two years, ratcheting automatically to $125 million every year regardless of deficits, police and fire needs, or the condition of the streets, while expanding taxpayer-funded down payment loans to households earning twice the area median income.
Measure D (NO)
‍ San Francisco Ballot Access Restriction Measure
Quadruples the signatures ordinary citizens need to put a measure on the ballot, from 2 percent to 8 percent of registered voters, pricing grassroots taxpayer groups and neighborhood coalitions out of the initiative process while leaving well-funded special interests free to simply buy the signatures they need.
Measure E (NO)
‍ San Francisco Contracting Power Transfer
Hands an unelected City Administrator rulemaking power that takes effect unless politicians affirmatively stop it, raising the dollar threshold above which elected supervisors must approve a contract, and extending that same official's term.
Measure F (YES)
‍ San Francisco Executive Branch Accountability Measure
Gives the mayor San Franciscans actually elect the authority to hire and fire the department heads responsible for city services, including — by the measure's own findings — department heads implicated in serious ethical lapses whom the mayor currently cannot remove.
Measure G (YES)
‍ San Francisco Great Highway Vehicle Access Restoration
Reopens a taxpayer-funded arterial to the taxpayers who paid for it, undoing a closure that pushed traffic onto Sunset District residential streets and cut off seniors, parents and everyone who cannot make the trip by bicycle.
Measure H (NO)
San Francisco Property Tax Increase
Imposes a new costly property tax despite massive levels of wasteful spending, giving government workers massive salary hikes and pension spikes, and awarding contracts to political supporters without fair and open competition.
Measure I (NO)
‍ San Francisco County Transfer Tax Increase
Imposes a new costly transfer tax despite massive levels of wasteful spending, giving government workers massive salary hikes and pension spikes, and awarding contracts to political supporters without fair and open competition.
Measure J (NO)
San Francisco Repeal of Exemptions for Transfer Tax (i.e. Tax Increase)
Slaps San Francisco’s real estate transfer tax—up to 6% of fair market value—on foreclosed commercial properties and apartment buildings with five or more units, even when a lender is merely taking back a distressed property. Turns business failures and foreclosures into an estimated $100 million to $150 million annual City Hall windfall while making troubled properties even more expensive to resolve and return to productive use
Measure RTM (No)
‍ Regional Sales Tax Increase
Imposes a new costly sales tax despite massive levels of wasteful spending, giving government workers massive salary hikes and pension spikes, and awarding contracts to political supporters without fair and open competition.
