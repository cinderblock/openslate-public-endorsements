Local Ballot Measures
Measure C (NO)
‍ Santa Maria Permanent Sales Tax Increase
Imposes a permanent half-cent sales tax with no expiration date, and despite a ballot question listing parks, the library, youth programs and roads, this is a general tax whose $13 million a year lands in the general fund where the Council can spend it on anything — including the salaries and pension obligations that created the shortfall in the first place
Measure D (NO)
‍ Santa Ynez Valley High School Property Tax Increase
Imposes a new costly property tax despite massive levels of wasteful spending, giving government workers massive salary hikes and pension spikes, and awarding contracts to political supporters without fair and open competition.
Measure E (NO)
‍ Isla Vista Utility Tax
Taxes the bills nobody can opt out of — electricity, water, trash, gas — in a community of students and low-wage renters who already pay some of the highest housing costs in California, and pairs it with a companion measure raising the district's spending cap because the tax alone would exceed what the district is legally allowed to spend.
‍ Measure F (NO)
‍ Isla Vista Spending Cap Increase
The quiet half of a two-part tax package: Measure E collects the money and this one raises the constitutional spending cap so the district can actually spend it, because a tax that exceeds the Gann limit has to be refunded to the people who paid it.
Measure G (NO)
‍ Guadalupe Business Tax
Imposes a new costly business tax despite that will destroy jobs to simply support more massive levels of wasteful spending by the city.
Measure H (NO)
‍ Allan Hancock Joint Community College District Property Tax Increase
Imposes a new costly property tax despite massive levels of wasteful spending, giving government workers massive salary hikes and pension spikes, and awarding contracts to political supporters without fair and open competition.
Measure I (NO)
‍ Carpinteria Permanent Sales Tax Increase
Extends and increase costly sales, WITH NO END DATE, tax despite massive levels of wasteful spending, giving government workers massive salary hikes and pension spikes, and awarding contracts to political supporters without fair and open competition.
Measure J (NO)
‍ Guadalupe Hotel and Campground Tax Doubling
Doubles the lodging tax rate and extends it for the first time to RV parks and campgrounds — taxing retirees and working families on a camping trip, not the business travelers the "visitors pay it" pitch imagines — to raise about $20,000 a year, permanently.
