## Santa Ana Mayor

- **Non-Partisan** | **Dan Shimell**

  > Elect Dan Shimell as Mayor to keep Santa Ana on the right track for progress. Progressive endorsements: Shimell is endorsed by Chispa, Santa Ana Educators Association, Orange County PAC, Santa Ana City Council Members Benjamin Vazquez and Jessie Lopez, Santa Ana Unified School District (SAUSD) Board Vice President Hector Bustos, and SAUSD Board Member Valerie Magdaleno. Key issues: Housing that people can afford, standing with the immigrant community, and services for every neighbor. Governance and community leadership experience: Shimmell is an attorney who has represented people in state and federal courts, including on land use, environmental law, and municipal law. Other background: Shimmell earned a bachelor’s degree in political science from the University of California-San Diego, and a law degree from University of California Davis School of Law. The Race General Election: Three candidates are running in the November 3 general election: Valerie Amezcua (incumbent), Dan Shimell, and Dwayne Shipp. This is a nonpartisan race. Santa Ana residents demanded that Mayor Amezcua resign in 2025 after she condemned local protests against ICE raids and the National Guard occupation. She has also long faced criticism for her close relationship with the Santa Ana Police Officers Association, which has significantly funded her campaigns. The District City: Santa Ana is Orange County’s fourth most populous city. Santa Ana’s mayor and city council oversee the needs of 310,000 people and manage an estimated operating budget of $734 million annually. District demographics: As of the 2020 Census, Santa Ana had a demographic breakdown of 77% Latino, 12% Asian, and 1% Black. The Position Santa Ana uses a council-manager government structure, in which the mayor is elected at large and acts as chair of the six-member city council. The city council is responsible for appointing the city manager, establishing policy, passing local laws (called ordinances), voting on budget appropriations, and developing an overall vision for the city. The mayor of Santa Ana presides over council meetings and is an at-large representative of the city. In Santa Ana, a mayor is elected to a two-year term, with a limit of four consecutive terms. ## Santa Ana School Board, Board of Education Trustee, Area 4

- **** | **Katelyn Brazer Aceves**

  > Re-elect Trustee Katelyn Brazer Aceves to keep Santa Ana Unified School District on the right track for progress. Katelyn Brazer Aceves has served as a Santa Ana Unified School District Board (SAUSD) Trustee since 2022, when she was elected with 37% of the vote in a three-person race. During her tenure, she has led efforts to improve academic achievement, elevate student and parent voices, expand resources for families, strengthen fiscal transparency, and modernize schools. For instance, she led the creation of the district’s Immigration Support Plan and Family and Community Connection Advisory, and secured a new partnership with UC Irvine at Monroe Elementary. Brazer Aceves is endorsed by OC Action, UNITE HERE Local 11, California School Employees Association, Orange County Young Democrats, Orange County Supervisor Vicente Sarmiento, SAUSD Vice President Hector Bustos, and Santa Ana City Councilmembers Benjamin Vazquez and Jessie Lopez. The DistrictSchool system: Santa Ana Unified School District is located in Orange County, which is California’s third most populous county. Santa Ana Unified School District includes 11 high schools, 10 middle schools, and 34 elementary schools, serving a population of roughly 44,000 students. Governance structure: Santa Ana Unified School District has a five-person board that provides administrative oversight and manages a budget of $710 million annually.
