## Oakland Mayor

- **Non-Partisan** | **Barbara Lee**

  > Re-elect Barbara Lee to put Oakland on the right track for progress. Progressive endorsements: Lee was previously endorsed by some groups, including the Alameda Labor Council AFL-CIO, SEIU 1021, California Nurses Association, California Working Families Party, and Our Revolution East Bay. She has also received the endorsement of local and state leaders, including Dolores Huerta, City Councilmember Kevin Jenkins, former Gov. Jerry Brown, State Sen. Jesse Arreguin, Asm. Mia Bonta, and Rep. Lateefah Simon. Top issues: A comprehensive, community-focused approach to public safety, investing in effective solutions that prevent homelessness before it starts, attracting new investment and creating jobs, protecting Oaklanders from illegal persecution and infringement on civil rights, and investing in and expanding programs serving Oakland youth. Governance and community leadership experience: Lee has served as mayor since 2025, when she was elected with 53% of the vote. After her election, she launched a ten-point plan, much of which she accomplished in her first year in office, including securing funding for affordable housing, raising philanthropic investments to combat illegal dumping, introducing balanced budgets for 2025 and 2026, and reduced fire hazards. Mayor Lee has also led efforts to reform and streamline city operations to better serve residents. Under the Trump administration, she affirmed the city’s sanctuary city status and issued executive orders to prohibit ICE agents from using city-owned property. Last year, Lee created a new Office of Homeless Solutions to oversee work on encampments and shelters. Mayor Lee is behind a proposal to restructure Oakland’s government to a “strong mayor” system, which would give her power to vote legislation and portions of the city from the city council, and give her more control over city administration. The proposal was developed after a community engagement process, and is opposed by four of the eight city councilmembers. Oakland residents will vote on the Oakland Mayoral Powers, City Council Oversight, and Elected Officials Charter Amendment in the November 3 general election. Lee had a long career in politics before becoming Oakland mayor. She served in Congress from 1998–2025, California State Senate from 1992–1996, and the California State Assembly from 1990–1992. In her 26-year tenure in Congress, Rep. Lee was an outspoken progressive leader across various issues, including ending poverty, and has been a prolific author of legislation related to ending AIDS/HIV, malaria, and tuberculosis. She was the only member of Congress to vote against the authorization for the use of military force after the attacks on September 11, 2001, a controversial position at the time. Other background: Lee is from El Paso, TX, and moved to the San Fernando Valley as a teenager. She attended Mills College and has lived in the Bay Area her entire adult life. The RaceGeneral election: There are four candidates running in the November 3 general election: Barbara Lee (incumbent), Brenda Grisham, Mindy Pechenuk, and Julius Robinson. This is a nonpartisan race. Candidate fundraising: Lee’s campaign has raised $315,000. Opponent fundraising: Grisham’s campaign has raised $12,035, Pechenuk’s has raised $18,566, and Robinson’s campaign has not filed any financial disclosures. The DistrictCity: Oakland is Alameda County’s most populous city. Oakland’s mayor and city council oversee the needs of 435,000 people and manage an estimated operating budget of $1.7 billion annually. District demographics: As of the 2020 Census, Oakland had a demographic breakdown of 25% Latino, 16% Asian, and 21% Black. Recent election results: Alameda County, which includes Oakland, voted for Kamala Harris for president in 2024 by 54 points and for Gavin Newsom for governor in 2022 by 58 points. The PositionOakland is governed by a city council of eight elected representatives. A city council is responsible for establishing policy, passing local laws (ordinances), voting on budget appropriations, and developing an overall vision for the city. These bodies are often augmented by two additional seats held by a mayor and a vice mayor. Oakland uses the mayor-council government structure. The mayor is elected at large and acts as chair of the city council and the city’s chief executive officer. The Oakland mayor does not hold any special legislative power.
