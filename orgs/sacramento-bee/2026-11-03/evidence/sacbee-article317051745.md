Election Endorsements

The Bee endorsements: Measures I, J and K would reshape Folsom city government | Opinion

By The Sacramento Bee Editorial Board

October 6, 2026 5:00 AM

A Regional Transit light rail train prepares to leave downtown Folsom on Friday, Sept. 5, 2025. In the November general election, Folsom voters will consider three ballot measures to make significant changes in city government, One is Measure K, which would alter the powers of the Folsom Historic District Commission.

HECTOR AMEZCUA

hamezcua@sacbee.com

Folsom periodically updates its city charter, which establishes the rules for how the city is governed. This November, voters will consider three straightforward measures that all deserve approval.

Only Folsom voters can alter the city charter. Therefore, The Bee Editorial Board recommends “yes” votes on Measures I, J and K.

Currently, the democratically elected Folsom City Council does not hire and fire the city attorney. The authority belongs to the unelected city manager. Measure I would give councilmembers the authority to hire and fire the city attorney.

Measure J “would give the city council discretion to grant individual boards or commissions decision-making authority. This authority could be broad or limited to specific circumstances, as determined by the council.” The city council could also remove this authority to make a board or commission only advisory.

An impartial analysis of Measure J found that the city commission most likely to be affected is the Historic District Commission. The commission reviews development proposals in Folsom’s Historic District to help preserve and enhance its historic atmosphere and ensure new development is consistent with the district’s historical character, wrote City Attorney Sari Myers Dierking. The City Council currently makes all binding decisions affecting development in the Historic District. If Measure J passes and the commission receives decision-making authority, certain projects may proceed more quickly because separate City Council approval would not be required.

Measure K would increase campaign contribution limits in Folsom that have not been adjusted in 30 years. The current limit is $150, an amount Measure K would raise to $500, putting it far more in line with peer communities in the region.

We support Measure I because it makes sense for the city attorney to report to the councilmembers elected by Folsom residents. Approving Measure I would enshrine it in the city charter.

Measure J would give councilmembers more authority over city commissions. That makes sense because it would better connect decision-making authority with democratically elected leaders.

The reason we support Measure K is that outside money has played a disproportionate role in Folsom politics because the city too tightly restricts campaign contribution limits. Measure K would give Folsom greater say over its future by increasing contribution limits in line with other communities.

All three of these reforms originated from an ad hoc commission that laudably did its job.

City charters can need some fine-tuning to keep up with the times; Folsom voters should seize that opportunity this November.
