Roseville schools are growing fast. Their next trustee needs the right priorities | Opinion

By The Sacramento Bee Editorial Board

October 6, 2026 3:42 PM

A map shows the current attendance boundaries for Roseville Joint Union High School District campuses in Roseville. The existing boundaries have strained Roseville and West Park high schools as rapid growth in west Roseville pushes both campuses toward projected capacity in the coming years.

Roseville Joint Union High School District

In the last decade, Roseville has undergone substantial population growth.

Unlike other districts experiencing enrollment declines, the Roseville Joint Union High School District has struggled to manage capacity at some schools. At the same time, the district is deliberating on how to incorporate artificial intelligence into its curriculum while also managing the cellphone usage of students at its campuses.

These are complicated issues that would be well served by fresh ideas from new voices focused on students.

This is why The Sacramento Bee Editorial Board endorses Jeresia Ewell to be RJUHSD’s trustee for Area 2. A lawyer, Ewell is an engaged candidate and a resident of West Roseville. She has two kids, including a child enrolled at an RJUHSD school. Ewell owns a legal consulting firm and serves in a bar association leadership role.

Ewell is rightly skeptical of partisan politics that crept into school board discussions in recent years. In 2023, the RJUHSD school board cut ties with a prominent LGBTQ support group and adopted a Parents Rights and Responsibilities regulation, which requires the district to inform a parent if their child misses class to attend a counseling session or visit a campus wellness center or if a change occurs in a student’s records, such as a name change.

Some students and community members who spoke against the policy felt that it would infringe upon students’ privacy rights.

“I have had a lot of feedback about (the district’s) LGBTQIA community being underserved,” Ewell said. “I’ll be meeting with community leaders to hear about how their experiences have been in the district.”

Ewell said she would not have supported the policy had she been on the board in 2023. She said that her, “biggest concern is student safety.”

Her opponent is Pete Constant, an RJUHSD school board member for the past six years. Constant was the board president when the district approved its parents’ rights policy.

Ewell agrees that the district should continue to lead on the rigorous Advanced Placement and International Baccalaureate classes as well as the district’s career technical education program that sets students who might not be college-bound up for success. Each school in the district offers several distinct CTE programs, including in industry sectors such as engineering, health sciences and business.

But she wants the district to go slow on AI.

“Until we get a baseline on how to use AI responsibly, it’s going to be hard to fold into our curriculum,” Ewell said.

With growth and technology challenges facing the district, Ewell’s caution and common sense would make her a valuable addition to the RJUHSD board. She’s focused on students and safety, which is the right approach for a district that is growing fast.
