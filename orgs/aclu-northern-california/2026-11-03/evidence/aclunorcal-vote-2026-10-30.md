## NO on PROP 39 — Prohibits citizens from voting unless they present government-issued ID

Voting is a fundamental right, and every eligible Californian should be able to cast a ballot without unnecessary barriers.
Prop 39 would require voters to present a government-issued identification card for in-person voting and add a personal ID number on vote-by-mail envelopes. The measure is unclear on which kinds of ID would be accepted, creating an unnecessary barrier to the democratic process that will deny eligible voters access to the polls. These barriers would make it harder for eligible Californians to vote and put voter privacy at risk.
The burdens created by Prop 39 would especially affect voters who already face obstacles to participating in our democracy including voters of color, young voters, and voters with disabilities.
The ACLUs of California say NO on Prop 39. Californians should protect every eligible person’s freedom to vote–not make it harder to exercise that right.
Read more at noonprop39.com.

## YES on PROP 1 — Authorizes bonds for housing affordability programs

Housing is a human right.
Prop 1 would authorize an $11.25 billion bond to build and preserve affordable homes and expand homeownership opportunities. This represents the largest public investment in affordable housing in California history and will benefit all of us – with an equity lens towards veterans, seniors, low-and middle-income Californians, people with disabilities, working families, farmworkers, foster youth, and people experiencing houselessness.
Safe, stable housing gives people the foundation to work, raise families and fully participate in their communities.
The ACLUs of California say YES on Prop 1. California should invest in affordable housing that helps every person live with dignity and security.

## YES on PROP 3 — Provides permanent funding for schools and healthcare by extending existing tax on high incomes.

Every Californian deserves access to quality schools and health care.
Prop 3 would make an existing tax on California’s highest-income earners permanent, preserving $15 billion each year that currently funds public education and health care.
Strong public institutions expand opportunity and ensure that rights and freedoms are meaningful for everyone.
The ACLUs of California say Yes on Prop 3. California should continue investing in students, schools, and services that strengthen our communities.

## YES on PROP 4 — Repeals prohibition against public funding of election campaigns

Democracy works best when everyone has a fair opportunity to run for office.
Prop 4 would repeal California’s ban on public financing of campaigns, allowing the state to create systems that reduce the influence of wealthy donors and expand opportunities for candidates from all backgrounds.
Elections should be decided by voters–not by who can raise the most money.
The ACLUs of California say Yes on Prop 4. Public financing can help make California’s democracy more representative and accessible.

## NO on PROP 41 — Prohibits new state taxes that exclude revenues from state spending limit. Requires audits for new state special taxes.

Voter-approved investments should go where voters intended.
Prop 41 would create new constitutional requirements for statewide special taxes that could delay ballot measures and make it harder to fund voter-approved priorities like health care, education and other essential public services.
California already has safeguards to ensure accountability for public funds. We don’t need new barriers that make it harder to invest in our communities.
The ACLUs of California say NO on Prop 41. Voters should have the power to fund the services Californians rely on.

## NO on PROP 42 — Prohibits new state personal property taxes and certain retroactive state taxes

Californians should have the power to decide how to fund the services our communities need.
Prop 42 would permanently ban new state taxes on financial assets and other personal property, limiting options available to future voters and lawmakers to respond to crises, protect essential services and invest in our communities.
If passed, Prop 42 will exacerbate wealth inequality while starving state budgets of funding for healthcare, public education, infrastructure and emergency services – leaving middle-class taxpayers to pick up the tab through higher sales and income taxes.
The ACLUs of California say NO on Prop 42. California should preserve voters’ ability to make democratic choices about how we raise revenue and meet our communities’ needs.

## NO on PROP 43 — Limits voters’ ability to raise revenue for local government services.

Communities should have the power to invest in the services they need.
Prop 43 would require two-thirds voter-approval for local revenue measures that voters themselves put on the ballot. That means just one-third of voters could block what the rest of us want, making it harder to fund schools, affordable housing, health care and other community priorities.
By raising voter thresholds for citizen-led proposals, Prop 43 threatens essential neighborhood investments—including affordable housing, public transit, emergency services, and community parks. Prop 43 effectively ties the hands of residents trying to fund critical local needs. A minority of voters should not be able to block investments supported by the majority.
ACLU California Action says NO on Prop 43. California communities deserve the freedom to make local decisions that improve people’s lives.

## Local Ballot Measure Endorsements

NO on Los Altos Measure D: Measure D would mandate costly city-wide elections to approve each development project on city-owned parking plazas — creating new barriers to much-needed affordable housing in downtown Los Altos.
NO on Menlo Park Measure P: Housing development should not be contingent on a city vote. If passed, Measure P would create obstacles to affordable housing development by requiring a citywide vote on these projects. At a time of a housing affordability crisis, we need solutions, not obstacles to housing development. Learn more.
NO on San Francisco Measure F: Measure F directly undermines the authority and independence of two commissions – the Elections Commission and the Police Commission – where that independence is important to democracy and civil liberties. Measure F permits the removal of those commissioners without cause or process. Furthermore, Measure F would take away the Police Commission’s important power to fire the Chief of Police, and rests that power with the mayor alone, further eroding the Police Commission’s ability to hold the police department accountable. Read more on our blog.
YES on San Mateo County Measure U: Measure U would require that county supervisorial districts be redrawn every 10 years by an Independent Redistricting Commission. This reduces conflicts of interests and helps put community control into the redistricting process. Vote Yes on Measure U to increase transparency and public trust in the redistricting process. Learn more.
