STATEWIDE PROPOSITION ENDORSEMENTS
YES on:
Prop 1: Veterans and Affordable Housing Bond
Authorizes $11.25 billion in bonds for affordable housing, homeownership help, and veteran home loans, repaid from the existing state budget with no new taxes.
Prop 2: Save for California’s Future Act
Doubles the cap on the state’s rainy day fund, from 10% to 20% of General Fund taxes, so California can save more in good years and cushion downturns.
Prop 3: Permanent Funding for Schools and Healthcare
Makes permanent the current tax rates on the highest earners, set to expire in 2031, to protect funding for schools and healthcare, with no new tax rates.
Prop 4: California Fair Elections Act
Repeals the state ban on public campaign financing so state and local governments can create programs that reduce candidates’ reliance on big donors.
Prop 5: Recall of State Officers
Changes statewide recalls to a single question, whether to remove the official, and fills the vacancy afterward through the usual succession rules.
NO on:
Prop 39: Voter ID and Citizenship Verification
Adds new ID requirements for in-person and mail voting plus expanded citizenship checks, at a cost of tens of millions to low hundreds of millions of dollars a year.
Prop 41: Special Tax Audits and Gann Limit
Requires state audits of programs funded by new special taxes and bars new state taxes from being excluded from the state spending limit.
Prop 42: Constitutional Ban on Wealth and Retroactive Taxes
Writes into the Constitution a ban on new state taxes on personal property, including financial assets and retirement accounts, and limits retroactive state taxes.
Prop 43: Two-Thirds Vote for Local Special Taxes
Raises the vote needed to pass voter-proposed local special taxes from a majority to two-thirds starting in 2027, so a third of voters could block what most want.
Prop 44: Community Health Clinic Spending Requirements
Requires nonprofit community clinics to spend at least 90% of revenue on “program services” or pay a penalty, and the Legislative Analyst notes some clinics that can’t comply might close.
Prop 45: CEQA Streamlining for Essential Projects
Creates a fast track for environmental review of projects it defines as “essential,” including housing, water, and transportation, with firm deadlines and limits on court challenges.
