The largest number of registered voters in the 16th state Senate District live in Kern County, followed by Tulare County, Kings County and a small portion of Fresno County. The district is nearly 39% registered Democratic, about 30% Republican and 23% of voters have no party affiliation. As to demographics, the district is 73% Latino.

The district currently is represented in the state Senate by Melissa Hurtado, a Democrat, who is running for reelection. She faces Republican Guillermo Asunción Gonzalez in the Nov. 3 election. Hurtado and Gonzalez finished as the top two vote-getters in the June primary.

The November runoff election comes as politics in the district have been roiling since the 2021 redistricting that triggered a game of musical political chairs and one of the state’s closest elections in 2022, which Hurtado won.

Hurtado grew up in Sanger. The daughter of immigrant parents, she is the first in her family to graduate from college. She earned a bachelor’s degree in political science from Sacramento State University. Before entering elective office, she worked on issues involving teachers, working families, healthcare, affordability and retirement security.

In 2016, she was elected to the Sanger City Council and two years later, in 2018, she was elected the youngest woman in California history to serve in the state Senate.

Then came the 2021 redistricting, which moved district lines and left Hurtado competing for reelection against a fellow Democrat. Instead, she moved her official residence south to Bakersfield, where she competed successfully in the primary election against two Democrats and two Republicans. In the November runoff, she barely won her 2022 reelection, beating back a young Republican Porterville farmer by only 15 votes.

Two years later in 2024, just midway through her state Senate term, Hurtado decided to run for Congress to unseat incumbent Republican David Valadao. But so did former Democratic Assemblyman Rudy Salas. In the primary, Hurtado finished last and did not advance to the November runoff. Salas did. Valadao survived the challenge and went on to serve his present two-year term.

That brings us to the June 2026 primary, when Democrat Manpreet Kaur, a Bakersfield councilwoman who questioned Hurtado’s commitment to serving the district, unsuccessfully challenged the incumbent. Receiving enough votes in the June primary to compete in the Nov. 3 runoff, Hurtado now faces Republican Gonzalez, whose family runs two restaurants — The Idle Spur in McFarland and Guillermo’s Steakhouse in Delano.

Born in Bakersfield and raised in Mexico, Gonzalez writes on his campaign website that he grew up helping in his family’s restaurant. He also worked in construction and local shops. Until he decided to run for the state Senate this year, he was a field representative for Republican Congressman David Valadao.

What Gonzalez has never done is run for or held an elective public office. But Hurtado has. She now lives in Bakersfield and maintains a district office and staff in the city.

In the eight years she has represented Kern County, she has been an effective representative for the district and its residents. She has brought state resources and money to the area. She has been responsive to its needs. Hurtado should be reelected. Voters should not elect an untested and inexperienced candidate to be their voice in Sacramento.

Hurtado’s reelection has broad support from the California Democratic Party and organized labor, along with support from public-safety, education, health-care and other professional groups. She also has support from many Kern County elected officials.

Gonzalez is backed by the California Republican Party and conservative Republican organizations, with substantial independent spending from Republican-aligned political committees, as well as business representatives.

For more about the candidates, go to their websites gonzalezforsenate.com and melissahurtado.com.

### Nathan Magsig

12th STATE SENATE

The 12th state Senate District is spread across three counties. Kern has about 44% of the population, Fresno 38% and Tulare 18%. The largest population center is Bakersfield. Other Kern County cities in the district are California City, Ridgecrest, Taft and Tehachapi, as well as some other Kern cities. Fresno-Clovis is another major population center.

Republican state Sen. Shannon Grove now represents the district, which is dominated by Republican voter registration. She has reached her term limit and is not running for reelection in the Nov. 3 election.

Fresno County Supervisor Nathan Magsig, a Republican, and social worker William Brown Jr., a Libertarian, emerged from the June primary as the top two vote-getters to compete for the 12th District seat. Magsig received 59.5% of the vote to Brown’s 26.5%. Fellow Republican Louis Miramontes received only 14% and did not qualify for the runoff. The lopsided primary finish was a result of Magsig’s longstanding involvement in Fresno County and Clovis politics, and name recognition.

Magsig was born in Fresno County and attended Clovis schools. He earned a bachelor’s and master’s degree in criminology, and a master’s in business administration from Fresno State University. He has worked as a California legislative aide, youth pastor, executive director of a nonprofit affordable housing organization and energy director for the Fresno Economic Opportunities Commission. He is a licensed general contractor and energy management/building performance specialist.

He served 16 years on the Clovis City Council and in 2017, he was elected to his current seat on the Fresno County Board of Supervisors. Magsig’s public involvement has centered on water, agriculture, economic development, public safety and wildfire/forest management.

Born in Pensacola, Fla., and now a resident of Visalia, Brown is a social worker and Marine Corps veteran. His 13 years in the military were spent as an F/A-18 avionics technician. His public biography lists his occupation after the military as a social worker. Initially registered as a Democrat, Brown changed his registration to Libertarian in 2020. He has never served in an elective office.

Residents of the 12th state Senate District need an experienced and tested representative in Sacramento. They should elect Nathan Magsig.

For more about the candidates, go to their websites, nathanmagsig.com and williambrownsd12.com.
