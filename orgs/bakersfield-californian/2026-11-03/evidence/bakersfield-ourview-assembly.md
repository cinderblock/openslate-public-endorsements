Both political parties are investing support and resources into the 35th Assembly District campaigns of Bakersfield City Councilman Andrae Gonzales, a Democrat, and McFarland Mayor Saul Ayon, a Republican.

The 35th District seat opened up when incumbent Dr. Jasmeet Bains decided to run for Congress, instead of reelection, in the June primary. Failing to place as one of the top-two vote getters, she did not advance to the November Congress runoff and will leave her Assembly seat in December.

The 35th Assembly District is located entirely within Kern County. More than 50% of the city of Bakersfield, including east Bakersfield, as well as Delano, McFarland, Shafter, Wasco, Arvin and Lamont are in the district. Voter registration favors Democratic over Republican nearly two to one.

Growing up in McFarland and graduating from McFarland High School, Ayon spent nearly 25 years as a Kern County Sheriff’s deputy. After his 2020 retirement, he obtained a credential to teach criminal justice courses at McFarland High School. He earned an associate degree in criminal justice from Bakersfield College and a bachelor’s from Union Institute and University.

In 2020, Ayon was elected to the McFarland City Council and in 2022 he was elected mayor. Ayon is active in the local Republican Party. He currently is secretary of the Kern County Republican Central Committee.

His candidacy has been endorsed by several Republican officeholders and organizations, as well as the California Hispanic Legislative Caucus, the Howard Jarvis Taxpayers Association and Kern County law enforcement associations. Reform California, a conservative, tax-fighting organization founded by Assemblyman Carl DeMaio, R-San Diego, lists the 35th Assembly and Ayon’s candidacy as a Republican “pick up” opportunity.

During his four years on the City Council and as mayor, Ayon lists among his achievements eliminating a $2.9 million city budget deficit, rebuilding the police department and securing more than $60 million in grants for infrastructure, water and public safety.

Born and raised in Bakersfield, Gonzales graduated from East Bakersfield High School and earned a bachelor’s degree from UC Berkeley in political science. He has helped establish several local nonprofit organizations, including Faith in Action, Children First, the Kern Alliance of Nonprofits and Hub of Bakersfield. He currently is chief executive officer of Stewards Inc., a Kern County nonprofit that works with vulnerable individuals through connection to social services, resources and acting as an appointed representative payee service.

In 2010, he was elected to and served six years on the Bakersfield City School District Board of Trustees. In 2016, he was elected to the Bakersfield City Council. He represents Ward 2, which includes downtown, Oleander and east Bakersfield.

During his decade on the Bakersfield City Council, he can cite many accomplishments, including leading efforts effort to create the city’s first “low-barrier,” 300-bed homeless shelter; addressing housing needs through the creation of the city’s Affordable Housing Trust Fund and Bakersfield Community Land Trust; and helping downtown revitalization by attracting and retaining small businesses and initiating 18th and 19th Street corridor improvements.

As chairman of the Budget and Finance Committee, he helped build the city’s reserves and establish a trust fund to address unfunded pension liabilities. His candidate statement credits his council tenure with repaving numerous streets and alleys, adding sidewalks, and improving street lighting and parks in Ward 2.

Gonzales is backed by the California Democratic Party, organized labor, teachers and school administrators, housing advocates and a number of business groups, along with Democratic elected officials.

On their websites and in candidate statements, both Ayon and Gonzales demonstrate their commitment to responding to their constituents’ needs.

Ayon, who represents a city of about 15,000 residents, campaigns on public safety, lower taxes, agriculture and government accountability; Gonzales, who represents California’s ninth largest city of about 422,853, focuses on education, affordability, housing, healthcare and community investment.

Gonzales’ depth of experience and length of public service far exceeds Ayon’s. Recognized as a public servant willing to work with diverse groups to solve problems, Gonzales will be a strong voice for Kern County in Sacramento. Vote for Andrae Gonzales for 35th Assembly District.

For more information about the candidates, go to their websites, ayonforassembly.com and andraegonzales.com.

### David Couch

32nd DISTRICT

The 32nd Assembly District includes portions of Kern and Tulare counties. Major population areas are Visalia, Ridgecrest, Tehachapi and Bakersfield, including the communities of Oildale and Rosedale.

The district’s current assemblyman, Stan Ellis, was elected in February 2025 in a special election to replace then-Assemblyman Vince Fong, who was elected to Congress. Within a few months, Ellis announced he would not seek reelection and Republican David Couch declared his candidacy.

The 35th is a “safe Republican” district, where Republican registered voters outnumber Democrats nearly two to one. Couch is one of only four candidates out 80 who is unopposed in their campaigns for Assembly seats on the Nov. 3 ballot. He has represented much of the district as a member of the Bakersfield City Council from 1999 to 2012, and as a Kern County supervisor from 2013 to his current term.

A lifelong resident of Bakersfield, Couch earned a bachelor’s degree from San Jose State University in business administration, accounting. He has worked for more than 25 years in the financial services industry.

His Assembly campaign is consistent with his record as a councilman and supervisor — concern for cost of living, public safety, homelessness, agriculture, water, energy and economic development.

The 32nd Assembly District should be well-served by Couch’s representations and continued stewardship.
