Ballot Measures
Statewide ballot measures

Propositions & Measures
Proposition 1 — NO — California is already deeply in debt. The state should focus on getting its finances back in shape rather than borrowing another $11.25 billion from Wall Street.
Proposition 2 — NO — Now is not the time to gut state spending limits, nor to encourage Sacramento politicians to spend even more money they do not have.
Proposition 3 — NO — California taxes are already high enough. Voters should force the politicians to keep their promises to allow "temporary" taxes to sunset rather than making them permanent.
Proposition 4 — NO — Politicians should not be raiding taxpayer pockets to pay for their election and reelection campaigns.
Proposition 5 — NO — Voters should be given the choice to recall politicians who aren't doing their jobs.
Proposition 37 — NO — California can't even manage its own budget, let alone a $25 billion second mortgage lending program. Nor should California taxpayers be placed at risk to cover borrower defaults.
Proposition 38 — NO — While immunology and immunotherapy research are laudable, this proposition is just a money-grab by a single California university.
Proposition 39 — YES — Voter identification is essential to maintaining the integrity of our elections and preventing wholesale election fraud.
Proposition 40 — NO — The "wealth tax" starts with billionaires but is written to allow the Legislature to raid the assets of the middle class, including 401k retirement accounts, savings and home equity.
Proposition 41 — YES — Low-performing state programs should not be funded at the expense of higher-performing programs.
Proposition 42 — YES — Stop the politicians from retroactively taxing your savings, investments and retirement accounts!
Proposition 43 — YES — It should be more difficult, not easier, for the politicians to raise property taxes.
Proposition 44 — NO — The politicians should not control the budgets of healthcare clinics.
Proposition 45 — YES — Let's reduce the amount of red tape and make it easier for government infrastructure projects to be completed.
Irvine Measure D — NO — Ranked choice voting adds unnecessary complexity to the ballot and will make local elections even more confusing than they already are.

A "YES" recommendation means the Lincoln Club of Orange County urges a vote in favor of the measure; "NO" means a vote against.
