- **Non-Partisan** | **Kayla Lauriano**

## San Diego County Board of Supervisors, District 4

- **Non-Partisan** | **Monica Montgomery Steppe**

  > Re-elect Supervisor Monica Montgomery-Steppe to keep San Diego County on the right track for progress. Supervisor Monica Montgomery-Steppe has served in this seat since she won her 2023 special election with 62% of the vote. She is the first Black woman elected to the San Diego Board of Supervisors. In her time on the board, Sup. Montgomery-Steppe has focused on racial justice, homelessness solutions, behavioral health system reform, and improving community relationships with law enforcement. She led efforts to strengthen the Citizens’ Law Enforcement Review Board (CLERB) and worked on the California’s Reparations Task Force. Sup. Montgomery-Steppe previously served on the San Diego City Council, including as council president pro Tem, when she helped establish the Office of Race and Equity. She also worked as a civil rights attorney at the ACLU of San Diego and Imperial Counties and a senior policy advisor at City Hall. The Race General election: The primary election for this race was canceled. There are two candidates running in the November 3 general election: incumbent Supervisor Monica Montgomery-Steppe and Kristine Alessio. The Position Each of the 58 counties in California is governed by a five-person Board of Supervisors. A Board of Supervisors has legislative and executive power to manage county services and resources, including courts, jails, public health, and public lands. They also have quasi-judicial powers, which gives them the right to hold hearings, conduct investigations, and make decisions in a manner similar to judicial courts. Laws passed by Boards of Supervisors are generally called ordinances. Because counties include both incorporated cities, which are administered by their own city councils, and unincorporated areas, which are directly administered by the county, ordinances may or may not apply in different areas of the county. Supervisors are typically ‎limited to three terms, or 12 years in office total. ## San Diego City Council, District 2

- **Non-Partisan** | **Nicole Crosby**

  > Elect Nicole Crosby for City Council to keep San Diego on the right track for progress. Nicole Crosby serves as a deputy city attorney for the City of San Diego, where she prosecutes slumlords who exploit renters, and she has also prosecuted hate crimes, domestic violence, elder abuse, and gun crimes. She is also president of the Holmes Elementary PTA, and the immediate past president of the Clairemont Town Council. Crosby’s top issues include making San Diego more affordable; fixing the city’s potholes, sewers, sidewalks, and roads; prioritizing public safety and preventing gun violence; getting unhoused people off the streets and into supportive services; protecting the environment; protecting San Diegans from the federal government; finding common-sense solutions to the housing crisis; reining in short-term vacation rentals; and delivering affordable, accessible public transit. She has the endorsement of San Diego County Democratic Party, California Working Families Party, California Women’s List, San Diego Labor Democratic Club, San Diego and Imperial Counties Labor Council, Asm. Darshana Patel, Sup. Monica Montgomery Steppe, Sup. Paloma Aguirre, and Councilmember Kevin Shin. The Race June 2 primary election results: Richard Bailey 35%, Nicole Crosby 34%, Josh Coyne 13%, Mandy Havlik 11%, and three candidates who each won less than 5% of the vote. Richard Bailey and Nicole Crosby will contend in the November 3 general election. This is a nonpartisan race. The Position San Diego is governed by a nine-person city council. A city council is responsible for establishing policy, passing local laws (called ordinances), voting on budget appropriations, and developing an overall vision for the city. ## San Diego Unified School District, Member of the Board of Education

- **** | **Hayden Gore**

  > Elect Hayden Gore as Board Trustee to keep San Diego Unified School District on the right track for progress. Gore is a longtime bilingual educator who currently teaches at High Tech Middle Chula Vista. He co-founded the High Tech Education Collective to represent the more than 700 educators and support staff across High Tech High, served as its first president for four years, and led the union to its first contract. Gore previously taught as part of the Peace Corps and in rural schools in Guatemala, and directed the International Training Program for the Peruvian Forensic Anthropology Team. His top priorities are ensuring that schools are safe, inclusive, and nurturing spaces for all students, families, and staff; investing district resources in classrooms and student supports; and supporting efforts to develop affordable housing for educators, staff, and families on district land. Chris Ward, and four trustees of the San Diego Unified School District. The RaceGeneral election: Hayden Gore is running unopposed in the November 3 general election.
