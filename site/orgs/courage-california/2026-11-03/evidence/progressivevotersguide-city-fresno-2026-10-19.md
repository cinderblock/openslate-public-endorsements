## Fresno County Board of Supervisors, District 1

- **Non-Partisan** | **Maria Pacheco**

  > Elect Maria Pacheco for Supervisor to keep Fresno County on the right track for progress. Pacheco has been the Kerman mayor since 2024, and is the first Latina of Indigenous roots to serve in this role. She is also a senior field representative for State Assemblymember Esmeralda Soria. Pacheco has secured funding for public safety and parks, expanded educational and workforce pathways, and launched transportation programs for rural students. Her priorities are keeping communities safe, growing the local economy and creating good jobs, making housing more affordable, and protecting farming, water, and farm jobs. Pacheco is endorsed by several groups, including the National Association of Social Workers California, Dolores Huerta Action Fund, CAIR Action, Fresno Stonewall Democrats, San Joaquin Valley Democratic Club, Central Valley Progressive PAC, and Planned Parenthood Advocates Mar Monte. She is also supported by State Sen. Anna Caballero, Asm. Esmeralda Soria, Madera County Supervisor Leticia Gonzalez, San Joaquin Mayor Adam Flores-Cornejo, and San Joaquin Vice Mayor Martha Mejia. The Race June 2 primary election results: Mike Karbassi 33%, Maria Pacheco 29%, Omar Hernandez 12%, Eric Payne 11%, Lupe Flores 10%, and Felipe Perez 6%. Mike Karbassi and Maria Pacheco will contend in the November 3 general election. This is a nonpartisan race. The Position Each of the 58 counties in California is governed by a five-person board of supervisors. A Board of Supervisors has legislative and executive power to manage county services and resources, including courts, jails, public health, and public lands. They also have quasi-judicial powers, which gives them the right to hold hearings, conduct investigations, and make decisions in a manner similar to judicial courts. Laws passed by Boards of Supervisors are generally called ordinances. Because counties include both incorporated cities, which are administered by their own city councils, and unincorporated areas, which are directly administered by the county, ordinances may or may not apply in different areas of the county. ## Fresno Unified School District, Board of Education Trustee, Seat 4

- **** | **Veva Islas**

  > Re-elect Veva Islas as Trustee to keep Fresno Unified School District on the right track for progress. Islas has served on the Fresno Unified School District Board of Education since 2019, when she was elected with 45% of the vote in a three-candidate field, and is the current president. In her time on the board, she has championed parent engagement and language justice, expanded dual-enrollment opportunities, grown Career Technical Education programs, and expanded bilingual and biliteracy programs. Islas is also dedicated to early childhood education, improved school facilities, fiscal responsibility, and work-based learning opportunities. She is founder and executive director of Cultiva La Salud, a public health advocacy nonprofit in Fresno. She is endorsed by California Working Families Party, Fresno County Democratic Women’s Club, Planned Parenthood Advocates Mar Monte, Progressive PAC, National Women’s Political Caucus Fresno, Asm. Joaquin Arambula, and Fresno Unified School District Trustee Andy Levine.
