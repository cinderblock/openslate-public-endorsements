## San Diego County Board of Supervisors, District 4

- **Non-Partisan** | **Monica Montgomery Steppe**

  > Re-elect Supervisor Monica Montgomery-Steppe to keep San Diego County on the right track for progress. Supervisor Monica Montgomery-Steppe has served in this seat since she won her 2023 special election with 62% of the vote. She is the first Black woman elected to the San Diego Board of Supervisors. In her time on the board, Sup. Montgomery-Steppe has focused on racial justice, homelessness solutions, behavioral health system reform, and improving community relationships with law enforcement. She led efforts to strengthen the Citizens’ Law Enforcement Review Board (CLERB) and worked on the California’s Reparations Task Force. Sup. Montgomery-Steppe previously served on the San Diego City Council, including as council president pro Tem, when she helped establish the Office of Race and Equity. She also worked as a civil rights attorney at the ACLU of San Diego and Imperial Counties and a senior policy advisor at City Hall. The Race General election: The primary election for this race was canceled. There are two candidates running in the November 3 general election: incumbent Supervisor Monica Montgomery-Steppe and Kristine Alessio. The Position Each of the 58 counties in California is governed by a five-person Board of Supervisors. A Board of Supervisors has legislative and executive power to manage county services and resources, including courts, jails, public health, and public lands. They also have quasi-judicial powers, which gives them the right to hold hearings, conduct investigations, and make decisions in a manner similar to judicial courts. Laws passed by Boards of Supervisors are generally called ordinances. Because counties include both incorporated cities, which are administered by their own city councils, and unincorporated areas, which are directly administered by the county, ordinances may or may not apply in different areas of the county. Supervisors are typically ‎limited to three terms, or 12 years in office total. ## El Cajon Mayor

- **Non-Partisan** | **Alexander Kraft**

  > Elect Alexander Kraft for Mayor to put El Cajon on the right track for progress. Alexander Kraft is a community organizer and union leader. He has advocated for tenants’ rights with Barrio Logan residents, police reform with El Cajon residents, and the right to share food with the unhoused and immigrant safety during ICE kidnappings. Kraft is a member of SEIU Local 221, and has served as shop steward, chapter treasurer, bargaining team member, executive board member, and delegate to the county’s Labor Council. He previously worked as a middle and high school teacher. Kraft’s top issues include housing, jobs/labor, immigration, infrastructure, and public safety. Kraft has the endorsement of California Working Families Party, Democratic Socialists of America, the Green Party, SEIU Local 221, Latinos en Accion, and the Peace and Freedom Party San Diego. The Race General election: There are two candidates running in the November 3 general election: Alexander Kraft and Bill Wells (incumbent). This is a nonpartisan race.
