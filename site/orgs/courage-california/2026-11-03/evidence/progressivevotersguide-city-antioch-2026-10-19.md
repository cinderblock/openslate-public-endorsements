## Antioch City Council, District 1

- **Non-Partisan** | **Tamisha Torres-Walker**

  > Re-elect Councilmember Tamisha Torres-Walker to keep Antioch on the right track for progress. Progressive endorsements: Councilmember Torres-Walker has the endorsement of several groups, including Lift Up Contra Costa Action, Safe Return Action, Contra Costa Young Democrats, Contra Costa Labor Council, California Working Families Party, and Democratic Party of Contra Costa. Top issues: Affordable housing, safe neighborhoods, good jobs and a strong local economy, youth opportunity, transparent and accountable government, and one Antioch bringing neighborhoods together around shared values. Governance and community leadership experience: Councilmember Torres-Walker has served in this seat since 2020, when she defeated the incumbent, former Councilmember Joy Motts, with over 36% of the vote. She won her 2022 re-election over Motts by four votes. As a councilmember, Torres-Walker has expanded rent protections, advanced affordable housing solutions and compassionate, practical responses to homelessness, supported community-based violence prevention and improved emergency response, strengthened public oversight of government, and protected residents from pollution. She serves on the Racial Justice Oversight Body, Juvenile Justice Coordinating Council, DJJ Realignment Committee, Holistic Intervention Pretrial Committee, and Richmond Reimagining Public Safety Taskforce. Torres-Walker is the co-founder and executive director of Safe Return Project, where she focuses on the reintegration and empowerment of formerly incarcerated and convicted individuals. Other background: Torres-Walker earned her bachelor’s degree in psychology from California State University East Bay and has been a Soros Justice Fellow and Rosenberg Foundation Leading Edge Fund fellow. The Race General election: There are two candidates running in the November 3 general election: Tamisha Torres-Walker (incumbent) and Joy Motts. The District City: Antioch is Contra Costa County’s third most populous city. Antioch City Council District 1 includes the northern section of the city. Antioch is managed by a council-manager-structured government. The Position Incorporated cities in California are generally governed by a five-person city council. A city council is responsible for establishing policy, passing local laws (called ordinances), voting on budget appropriations, and developing an overall vision for the city. City councilmembers in Antioch serve four-year terms.
