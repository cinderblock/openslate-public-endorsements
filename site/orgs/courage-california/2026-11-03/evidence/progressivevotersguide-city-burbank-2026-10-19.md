Endorsements: Sheriff Robert Luna is endorsed by the Los Angeles Democratic Party, Planned Parenthood Advocacy Project Los Angeles County Action Fund, Los Angeles County Federation of Labor, UNITE HERE Local 11, and many other labor unions and Democratic clubs. Top priorities: Reduce violent crime, improve public trust and accountability, modernize the department, enhance employee wellness, and address homelessness Governance and community leadership experience: Luna has served as sheriff since 2022, when he won 61% of the vote. During his first term, he implemented some reforms, including creating the Office of Constitutional Policing and a policy banning law-enforcement gangs, enforcing the use of body cameras in jails and creating a unit within county jails to review use-of-force incidents, and added a new mental health assessment area to the inmate reception center downtown. However, in September 2025, California Attorney General Rob Bonta filed a lawsuit alleging unconstitutional, inhumane conditions inside county jails, and communities continue to complain of excessive use of force, including when people protested against ICE and the National Guard. Prior to his election as sheriff, Luna had a 36-year career with the Long Beach Police Department, in which he held positions of increasing rank until he was named chief in 2014. He holds a master’s in public administration from Cal State University-Long Beach, he graduated from the FBI National Executive Institute and National Academy, and he completed Harvard University’s program for senior executives in state and local government. Other background: Luna grew up in East Los Angeles and is a longtime resident of Long Beach. The Position Each of the 58 counties in California elects a sheriff to police unincorporated areas of a county, to manage county jails, and to act as security in local government buildings. The sheriff is a constitutionally elected official. A sheriff’s role can vary from county to county, but they tend to be sworn peace officers with the power to make arrests, serve before a magistrate or a judge, serve warrants for arrest, and give tickets and citations. Sheriffs are elected to four-year terms in office. ## Referral E

- **VOTE YES** | **Referral E** | VOTE YES ON MEASURE E

  > In 2024, voters passed Measure G to create an independent Ethics Commission, which is now being established by Los Angeles County. Measure E proposes a change to the Los Angeles County Charter that would provide a more structured framework to the Ethics Commission and its Ethics Compliance Officer, including guidance on appointment processes, qualifications, terms, and duties and responsibilities. It would be funded by a guaranteed minimum budget of $14.3 million a year. Measure E would also ensure that the County maintains its 10% general fund commitment to community investment and prioritizing alternatives to incarceration that voters approved with Measure J in 2020. Why voting YES on Measure E matters:Los Angeles County’s Ethics Commission is being established as an independent body, which means it should operate apart from any of the individuals or institutions that it may be required to investigate. VOTING YES will ensure an Ethics Compliance Officer selection process that is methodical and rooted in a commitment to ensuring full independence for this office. The purpose of the Ethics Commission is to review allegations of misconduct, increase government transparency, and contribute to the development and updating of ethics laws. To accomplish these objectives, the office must be transparent with and trustworthy to the public. VOTING YES will create parameters around the development of the Ethics Commission that establish meaningful accountability.A truly independent Ethics Commission has many benefits for local residents, including increased protection of tax dollars and a reduction in the cost of litigation and settlements that often result from government mismanagement. VOTING YES will support the establishment of an office that can provide effective oversight. Top support for Measure E:As of October 2026, there is no formal committee in support of Measure E. Voting yes has been endorsed by the Los Angeles County Democratic Party, League of Women Voters of LA County, California Common Cause, AAPI Equity Alliance, and Courage California.
