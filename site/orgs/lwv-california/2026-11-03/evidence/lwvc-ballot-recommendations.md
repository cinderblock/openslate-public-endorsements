## Verbatim page text (endorsement-bearing portions; "[...]" marks omitted body paragraphs; link targets in brackets)

```
Ballot Recommendations
Ballot Measure Recommendations
November 3, 2026 General Election
Below are the League of Women Voters of California’s recommendations** on the propositions appearing on the November 3, 2026 ballot. We base our recommendations on League positions developed through study and member consensus, and the LWVC Board of Directors carefully reviews each measure before taking a position.

Proposition 1
VETERANS & AFFORDABLE HOUSING BONDS
Funding from the 2018 bond for broad affordable housing programs has been spent, and there is a corresponding backlog of affordable housing projects stuck waiting for years for financing. Many families in California continue to endure high housing costs, leading to an increase in homelessness and overcrowding. Public subsidy is necessary to provide safe and stable housing for households at the lowest incomes.
[...]
LWVC RECOMMENDS:
Yes on Prop 1 Campaign [link: https://yesonprop1ca.com/]

Proposition 2
SAVE FOR CALIFORNIA’S FUTURE ACT
Proposition 2 will allow California to save more money during strong economic years so it can protect schools and other essential services when revenues fall.
[...]
LWVC RECOMMENDS:
Yes on Prop 2 Campaign [link: https://www.yesonprop2.com/]

Proposition 3
CHILDREN’S EDUCATION & HEALTH CARE PROTECTION
[...]
California should not give its highest-income residents a multi-billion-dollar tax cut while schools, health care, and other essential services face serious financial pressure. Prop 3 simply asks the top 2 percent to keep paying the same rates they already pay. The League strongly supports the measure because it protects vital services, helps California weather economic downturns, and keeps the state’s tax system fair.
LWVC RECOMMENDS:
Yes on Prop 3 Campaign [link: https://www.yesprop3ca.com/]

Proposition 4
CALIFORNIA FAIR ELECTIONS ACT
Elections should be won, not bought. But in California, personal wealth and access to big donors have become unofficial qualifications for public office. [...]
Prop 4 gives Californians a way to change that. It lifts California’s near-total ban on public campaign financing and allows the state and local governments to create voluntary programs. [...]
Prop 4 gives every California community the freedom to build elections where voters have more power and wealthy donors and special interests have less.
LWVC RECOMMENDS:
Yes on Prop 4 Campaign [link: https://www.cafairelections.org/]

Proposition 5
RECALL ELECTION REFORM FOR STATEWIDE OFFICERS
[...]
Prop 5 restores the recall to its proper purpose: deciding whether an official should remain in office. [...]
This is a matter of basic fairness and majority rule. Prop 5 protects accountability while ending a system that can hand power to a candidate most voters never chose.
LWVC RECOMMENDS:
Yes on Prop 5 Campaign [link: https://fixtherecall.com/]

Proposition 37
LOAN PROGRAM FOR MIDDLE-INCOME BUYERS OF NEW HOMES
[...]
Prop 37 could help a family buy a home without making it affordable to keep. [...]
California needs homeownership programs that lower families’ total costs, confront racial barriers to building wealth, and support strong communities. Prop 37 does not meet that test.
LWVC RECOMMENDS:
[icon: OPPOSE.svg; no campaign link]

Proposition 38
BONDS FOR IMMUNOLOGY MEDICAL RESEARCH
Medical research can save lives, but Prop 38 is the wrong way to fund it. [...]
Borrowing is a poor way to pay for ongoing scientific research. [...]
California can support important medical research without committing taxpayers to decades of debt and restricting future choices about where limited public dollars can do the most good.
LWVC RECOMMENDS:
No on Prop 38 Campaign [link: https://votenoonprop38.org/]

Proposition 39
PROHIBITS VOTING WITHOUT GOVERNMENT-ISSUED ID
Prop 39 is a partisan power grab to further President Trump’s voter suppression efforts. It is based on lies about our elections. Prop 39 is intended to make it harder to vote and easier for politicians to decide which voters count. Right-wing billionaires spent millions to put it on the ballot.
[...]
Don’t let President Trump and right-wing billionaires decide whose vote counts.
LWVC RECOMMENDS:
No on Prop 39 Campaign [link: https://noonprop39.com/]

Proposition 40
BILLIONAIRE TAX
Prop 40 would tax extreme wealth to help meet urgent needs, but its one-time structure conflicts with principles of sound public finance and risks long-term revenue loss.
[...]
The human need is urgent and asking those most able to pay to fill a hole the federal government created is a fair demand. But the tax mechanism limits legislative flexibility for allocating money where it is most crucial and raises the risk of taxpayer flight. Because we have strong positions on both sides of this measure, the League takes a neutral position on Prop 40.
LWVC RECOMMENDS:
[icon: Neutral.svg; no campaign link]

Proposition 41
LIMITS NEW STATE TAXES & REQUIRES SPECIAL TAX AUDITS
Prop 41 would put revenue from new state taxes under the Gann Limit’s rigid constitutional spending cap, making it harder to fund essential services and respond to changing needs. It would apply to any state tax enacted or taking effect on or after January 1, 2026.
The League opposed the Gann Initiative in 1979 because its spending limit was too inflexible to assure adequate government services in an uncertain future. Prop 41 would extend that constraint to new tax revenue. [...]
LWVC RECOMMENDS:
[icon: OPPOSE.svg; no campaign link]

Proposition 42
LIMITS NEW STATE TAXES ON PERSONAL PROPERTY
Prop 42 would lock broad new restrictions on state taxing power into the California Constitution, limiting California’s ability to respond to future needs.
[...]
LWVC RECOMMENDS:
[icon: OPPOSE.svg; no campaign link]

Proposition 43
LIMITS VOTERS’ ABILITY TO RAISE REVENUES FOR LOCAL GOVERNMENT SERVICES
Prop 43 would make it harder for communities to raise revenue for public services by allowing a minority of voters to block local tax measures. It would amend the Constitution to require a two-thirds vote for any new, increased, or extended special tax proposed by local voters through the initiative process. A simple majority keeps these decisions with most voters and gives communities more flexibility to respond to changing needs.
LWVC RECOMMENDS:
No on Prop 43 Campaign [link: https://www.noprop43ca.com/]

Proposition 44
90% HEALTH CARE SPENDING FOR NONPROFIT SAFETY-NET CLINICS
Prop 44 could reduce access to health care for Californians who already have the fewest options. [...]
A rule intended to direct more money to patient care should not put the safety net itself at risk. Prop 44 could worsen health disparities rather than reduce them.
LWVC RECOMMENDS:
No on Prop 44 Campaign [link: https://noprop44.com/]

Proposition 45
MODIFIES ENVIRONMENTAL REVIEW FOR CERTAIN PROJECTS
[...]
At the same time, the League is aware that CEQA has been used to delay, if not completely derail, large projects. The League has supported some streamlining of CEQA requirements. But the process overhaul of CEQA proposed by Prop 45 goes too far.
[...]
LWVC RECOMMENDS:
No on Prop 45 Campaign [link: https://www.noprop45ca.com/]
```
