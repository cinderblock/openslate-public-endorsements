Physician Voter Guide: 2026 General Election
Elections matter to physicians, their patients, and the
future of health care. As California prepares for the November 3 General
Election, our Physician Voter Guide provides information on CMA-endorsed
candidates and ballot measures to help physicians stay informed and engaged.
Statewide Offices
Below are the statewide races in which CMA has made an endorsement.
Governor
Xavier Becerra, former Secretary of the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services, has demonstrated a deep and sustained commitment to improving access to care, protecting patients and supporting physicians throughout his career. With our healthcare system facing mounting pressures, Becerra brings the leadership and experience needed for this moment.
Attorney General
Rob Bonta has been a steadfast champion for public health and physician autonomy. As Attorney General, he has defended reproductive health access; pushed back against Trump administration efforts to freeze funding for academic research and public health and safety net programs; supported public health-based responses to gun violence; and worked to uphold and strengthen corporate bar laws, ensuring medical decisions remain between doctors and patients rather than private equity groups.
Lieutenant Governor
Fiona Ma currently holds the office of State Treasurer, where she oversees financing and funding programs affecting hospitals and health care providers. Throughout her career, she has championed the critical financing programs and legislation that protect and expand patients' access to care.
State Treasurer
Eleni Kounalakis will bring a combination of financial expertise and public sector experience to the State Treasurer’s office. As Lieutenant Governor and as a former businesswoman and diplomat, she has demonstrated a focus on using public resources to expand opportunity and strengthen communities.

Statewide Ballot Measures
 Proposition 3 - VOTE YES
Prop. 3 would permanently extend the existing income tax rates on California's highest-income earners, preserving an established source of ongoing state revenue. California has relied on this revenue to maintain funding for essential services, including health care, and avoid cuts when the state faces budget challenges. Prop. 3 Fact Sheet
 Proposition 39 - VOTE NO
CMA opposes Prop. 39 because voting is a social determinant of health. Civic participation helps voters shape the policies and systems that directly impact their health. However, Prop. 39’s new requirement would make it harder to vote and would disproportionately impact Californians who already face obstacles to exercising their right to vote, including people with disabilities, naturalized citizens and historically underrepresented communities. Prop. 39 Fact Sheet
 Proposition 40 - VOTE NO
CMA opposes Prop. 40 because our health care system needs stable, sustainable funding, not a one-time infusion. While the measure claims to fund health care, it lacks accountability, does not guarantee that any new revenue will result in more health care spending and could actually result in health care funding being reduced and backfilled with Prop 40 revenue. Prop. 40 Fact Sheet
 Proposition 44 - VOTE NO
CMA opposes Prop. 44 because it undermines the community clinics that millions of Californians rely on. The measure’s stringent spending regulations on safety-net clinics will divert an estimated $1.7 billion away from clinics through crippling financial penalties. The measure would worsen the health care crisis our state faces and lead to overcrowding at emergency departments by forcing clinics to reduce services, lay off staff or shut their doors entirely. Prop. 44 Fact Sheet
