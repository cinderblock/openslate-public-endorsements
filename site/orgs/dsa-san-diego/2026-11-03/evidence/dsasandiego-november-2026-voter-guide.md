## Definitions
Endorsed
DSA endorsements are special. They require a chapter’s whole membership to vote to endorse a candidate and include a commitment by the chapter to devote time and resources to that candidate’s campaign. That means substantial energy is devoted to field organizing including neighborhood canvasses, phone, and textbanking. Endorsements therefore aren’t handed out to anyone; they’re reserved for candidates we really believe in.
Recommended
Sometimes a candidate closely aligns with us but for one or more of a number of reasons, is not endorsed. Maybe they didn’t seek an endorsement, or maybe we don’t have the capacity to support their endorsement. Sometimes a candidate may not be a socialist but we believe some parts of their platform will materially benefit the working class. Sometimes we recommend a candidate if the alternatives are much worse. It’s best to read the write-up to understand how enthusiastic our recommendation is.
No recommendation
Choosing not to recommend any candidate in a race is still a decision. Some write-ups will lay out our rationale for refusing to recommend any one candidate. Not being able to identify a good candidate in a race should trouble us all. Consider joining us and using that energy to get candidates we can be proud of in office.

### US Representative 51st District: Sara Jacobs
The outcome of the race in the 51st Congressional District is not in doubt: Sara Jacobs is expected to cruise to an easy reelection over Ricardo Cabrera, a Republican mounting a purely symbolic challenge. Normally, we would not bother with a recommendation in a race like this, but Jacobs – while no socialist – has proven herself to be one of the more progressive members of Congress from California (and by far the most progressive from San Diego County). She introduced the Block the Bombs Act (H.R. 3565), is an original co-sponsor of the Medicare for All Act (H.R. 3069), voted against the GOP’s most recent red-baiting anti-socialism resolution (H.Res. 58 – she voted present two years earlier on a similar resolution, H. Res. 9), has voted for every War Powers Act resolution to end the illegal war against Iran, is an original co-sponsor of the Green New Deal (H. Res. 332), and – despite being one of the wealthiest members of Congress – introduced the LEGACY Act (H.R. 4330) to slash the federal estate tax exemption in order to fund childcare for working families.
There are places where she could be better. For one, she is not a co-sponsor of the Colleges for All Act (H.R. 3543) to pay for tuition-free public four-year colleges for working-class and middle-class students. And she has not come out in support of Prop. 40, the Billionaire Tax Act. (We’re hoping to have the opportunity to amend this writeup before election day – come on, Rep. Jacobs. It’s a moral issue.) Nevertheless, we think it is important to recognize where she’s been closely aligned.
We recommend a vote for Sara Jacobs.

### Measure A: Yes
This measure is a package of proposed reforms to the governing charter of the county of San Diego. Reform packages can be uniquely opaque to voters because substantive policy changes are delivered to voters alongside procedural and bureaucratic adjustments. For instance, this measure proposes significant structural changes alongside capitalizing defined words for syntactical consistency—hardly a proposal most voters can be expected to parse. On balance, the procedural and substantive changes proposed move the County in a positive direction towards increased transparency and an empowered public.
There are two key provisions we think are worth noting. First, this measure would change the term limits for the board of supervisors, increasing the maximum number of terms a supervisor can serve from two to three. While a proposal to increase term limits might feel like a power grab, it really returns power to the electorate. Elections are term limits, and effective ones at that, so allowing an elected official to run for a third term gives voters more power over their own representation, allowing us to continue choosing effective leadership beyond arbitrary timelines.
Second, this measure would empower the Board of Supervisors to review top appointments at the County, reclaiming some authority from the Chief Administrative Officer who executes the business of the County. Giving hiring and firing power for additional, highly impactful positions to elected representatives, instead of resting solely with the CAO, should establish more accountability to the public for those roles.
These two significant pillars are accompanied by the formation of an ethics commission, new requirements for financial disclosure, and rule clarifications. In an effort not to let the perfect be the enemy of the good, we recommend a Yes vote on Measure A.
### Measure B: Yes
This measure is a half-cent sales tax increase on retail purchases. The revenue generated will be placed into a new fund, called the Protect San Diego’s Health and Safety Fund, and allocated in support of providing affordable healthcare, nutrition, and family services (60%), programs related to toxic sewage in the Tijuana River Valley (22.5%), public safety services (17.5%) and general administrative costs (1.5%). It is estimated to raise about $400 million annually and would increase the county sales tax rate floor from 7.75% to 8.25%.
Sales taxes are regressive and one of our least favorite taxes. However, the County’s sales tax rate is significantly lower than many comparable California areas, even with the increase. Plus, the funds are critical—federal cuts from the Trump administration have made it necessary for the County to fill gaps it unequivocally does not have the resources to fill without changes like a tax increase. While the measure does not contain the ever-elusive solution to the Tijuana sewage crisis or singlehandedly solve SNAP cuts, it is a crucial opportunity to generate local revenue to keep people and communities alive and healthy in the coming years. We recommend a Yes vote on Measure B.
### School Bond Measures G, M, N: Yes
There are multiple school bond measures on the ballot this November, but only three of them are in districts which have negotiated Project Labor Agreements (“PLAs”) with the San Diego County Building and Construction Trades Council to ensure that the work is performed with union labor. PLAs help ensure that the work is done with highly-trained local workers who are protected by a union contract, rather than by unscrupulous contractors trying to fleece public agencies. Find our write-ups for G here, M here, and N here.
We recommend a YES vote on Measures G, M, and N.

### City Council District 4: Henry Foster III
D4 is the southeastern corner of the city. It includes the neighborhoods of Chollas Park, Encanto, Emerald Hills, Skyline, and Valencia Park. It is currently represented by Henry Foster III, who is running for re-election. Foster has been a fairly reliable vote in the progressive bloc of City Council. He delivered a full-throated opposition to Flock in late 2025 and throughout budget balancing season this year he continuously banged the table to preserve public services like libraries, recreation centers, homelessness programs, and arts funding. His vote against Sean Elo-Rivera for the IHRA anti-semitism definition was certainly disappointing, but he has reliably gone to bat for his community in District 4. He has an all-star cast of endorsements including Monica Montgomery Steppe, Sean Elo-Rivera, Working Families Party, and the Labor Council.
Foster is being challenged by nurse/local activist, Martha Abraham. Much of Abraham’s name recognition in D4 stems from her outspoken opposition to “Footnote 7” in 2021. This footnote in the Land Development Code Update allowed denser development in the historically underserved District 4. Footnote 7 was successfully repealed by a unanimous City Council vote (including Foster) in 2025, but the spectacle inspired Abraham to launch a City Council campaign. Abraham’s official platform is very vague. However, she has come out in favor of warrantless surveillance via ALPRs and maintaining San Diego PD’s contract with Flock Safety. Additionally, a cast of unsavory characters is aligning behind Abraham, including San Diego United Communities (an anti-housing group that is also supporting Richard Bailey), the Lincoln Club, the San Diego Chamber of Commerce, and Mayor Todd Gloria. Notably, the San Diego Firefighters recently pulled their endorsement from Foster and switched it to Abraham; journalist Scott Lewis theorizes that this is an attempt to punish the city to resolve a contract dispute.
Foster is a progressive ally that we want to keep in City Council. We recommend voting for Henry Foster III.

## Oceanside
### City Council District 1: Eric Joyce
Oceanside District 1 covers downtown north of Oceanside Blvd and the Eastside, Loma Alta, and Oceana neighborhoods. Incumbent Eric Joyce is on the ballot again for a second term. Joyce was previously an Oceanside Unified School Board member and a teacher. He has consistently been the most progressive vote on the city council and has a record of advocating for working class interests.
While Joyce has been unable to make much progress during his first term because of a developer-friendly council (see the D2 race), his efforts have not gone unnoticed. He co-led initiatives to pass renter protections and respond to immigration enforcement, although both unfortunately failed. During a recent city council vote to approve a grant for ALPR camera maintenance, Joyce was the only councilmember to vote no and speak out against mass surveillance. Joyce supports mixed-income public housing, more affordable housing, and wants to protect working class residents from displacement. These are important priorities given the increasing gentrification of his district. He is endorsed by the Working Families Party, SEIU-UHW, SD Building and Construction Trades, UFCW local 135, and San Diego City Councilmember Sean Elo-Rivera.
Mike Cline, a military veteran who is endorsed by Carl DeMaio, is making a last-ditch effort against Joyce. His approach to affordability references responsible government spending, and his other policy priorities are public safety and the beach.
Eric Joyce might not call himself a democratic socialist, but he’s the closest thing we have on the Oceanside city council. We recommend a vote for Eric Joyce for Oceanside City Council, District 1.
### City Council District 2: Emily Gonzales
District 2 is northeast oceanside, covering the Libby Lake and Valley neighborhoods and bordering Camp Pendleton. Incumbent Rick Robinson is being challenged by four candidates, two of which are on the left.
While Rick Robinson claims to be an independent, he is endorsed by Carl DeMaio and has a conservative voting record. The former fire fighter and veteran has earned a reputation for controversial campaign contributions from developers and businesses. He recently violated state campaign finance laws, with his excuse being “I didn’t know.” Not the first time Robinson has played dumb. After listening to hours of testimony from Oceanside residents, he voted to support renter protections (a 3-2 vote), then backtracked during the subsequent deliberation saying “I didn’t realize what I had voted for,” causing the proposal to ultimately fail.
The Oceanside Democratic clubs found the perfect candidate to flip this seat in Emily Gonzales, a military spouse and nonprofit executive who joined the Oceanside Planning Commission one year ago. Gonzales has a progressive policy platform, although she is vague about policy details and some of her social media activity indicates she may be pro-police. She is clearly passionate about holding developers accountable, and has been organizing with community members around the College Street bridge closure (where developers have delayed construction). Gonzales is endorsed by the San Diego and Imperial Counties Labor Council and the Working Families Party, and has been very actively campaigning in the district.
Challenging Gonzales for the Democratic vote is Oscarin Ortega, an Oceanside native and founder of the nonprofit Lived Experiences. He has come out strong against ICE, developers, and the political establishment in Oceanside. He supports public housing over privately-led development, expanding free programs for youth, and restoring stewardship of the San Luis Rey River to the Payomkawichum and Kumeyaay Nations. Ortega is well-known in the community: both for good and bad. The Lived Experience office was shut down earlier this year because of a zoning issue, which seems to have disrupted his campaign activities.
Ortega’s entry into the race caused a lot of drama, with understandable concerns about splitting the vote. We believe Gonzales is the most viable challenge to Robinson and because of her strong labor backing, we recommend a vote for Emily Gonzales for Oceanside City Council, District 2.

### Measure R: Yes
Measure R, the “Fair Wages” ballot measure, is an essential update to Oceanside’s city charter to make it align with California state law. As required by the state, Measure R codifies fair prevailing wage requirements for City projects and allows the use of project labor agreements (PLAs) for public works projects. Currently, Oceanside’s charter is not in line with state law, which could lead to the city losing millions of dollars in infrastructure funds; this measure is therefore essential to preserve city infrastructure. Measure R is endorsed by the San Diego Building and Construction Trades Council and the San Diego and Imperial Counties Labor Council. We recommend a Yes vote on Measure R.
### Measure S: Yes
Measure S is an additional half-cent sales tax for the next 10 years (on top of the recently passed Measure X sales tax), exempting basic needs such as groceries, feminine hygiene products, diapers, and prescription medications. The tax is estimated to raise $20M per year for the City, which could theoretically be used for any general purpose by the City Council and mayor. The City has signaled that it plans to use the funds for the RE:BEACH coastal restoration project, pier bridge renovation, roadway maintenance, and construction of a new fire and police station. It would have similar oversight as Measure X, with a local citizen oversight committee.
While sales taxes are inherently regressive, this half-cent sales tax would bring Oceanside in line with eight other cities in San Diego. Additionally, local labor groups have endorsed the measure, as the additional funds will be used to support union-maintained infrastructure projects. We therefore recommend a Yes vote on Measure S.
## Vista
### Mayor: Kenneth Zuniga
The city of Vista has an interesting race for mayor, originally expected to be uncontested. Former Council Member Joe Green, a former Republican now running as an Independent, who believes that politics has no place in local government, is backed by Reform California, Carl DeMaio’s MAGA conservative political group.
Democratic Socialist Kenneth Zuniga is a community organizer and DSA San Diego’s endorsed candidate. He worked with the TRUST SD coalition and other community organizations to keep Flock and Automated License Plate Readers out of Vista, making it the only city in San Diego County without them. As a member of the Vista Homelessness Commission, he is working to update the City’s strategic plan to address homelessness.

## Grossmont Union High School District
The recent news cycle surrounding the Grossmont Union High School Board reads less like that of a mid-sized school board than it does a MAGA-infused fiefdom. Allegations include secretive messaging, racially-charged comments, LGBTQ discrimination, firings, lawsuits, school prayer, and limits on protestors.
This election cycle, constituents will have the chance to vote out two members of this embroiled board and replace another in what many see as an opportunity to bring some sense of reason back to a district of roughly 16,000 students.
We’ll list our recommendations on each of the three seats up for grabs below, but first is an abbreviated summary of why this board has been in the news for all the wrong reasons.
In 2025, despite protests from students and parents, the board voted to remove 61 school employees, including all of its librarians. Months later, the San Diego Union Tribune first reported that school trustees allegedly used private text and email threads to discuss official board business, reportedly naming allies to elevate to leadership positions and plotting against those deemed to be disloyal. The fallout prompted public calls for members of the board to resign.
In late 2025, a former district special education teacher was awarded $700,000 in a civil suit where she alleged that she was harassed over her sexual identity, retaliated against by the board, and called part of an “LGBTQ coven” by trustee Jim Kelly, who is up for reelection this November in Seat 5.
This past June, budget controversy erupted again after the board voted to add nearly $4 million to the school’s athletic coffers, a move that angered many still reeling after the cuts to librarians and other staff. Adding more fuel to fire, Kelly was accused of making racially-charged remarks at a board meeting defending the budget.
After teachers spent three years developing a proposed ethnic studies curriculum, Board President Gary Woods was criticized when he proposed an alternative program developed by a libertarian think tank during a board meeting. The board also received pushback after a proposal that would have potentially loosened restrictions around school prayer and another measure to limit where protestors could stand during board meetings. The board eventually pulled back from both proposals.
Given this fantastically long list of controversies, we are recommending all three board candidates backed by the Grossmont Education Association, as we see each as a better representative than their respective incumbents or opposition. Details on each race are below.

She promises commitments to school safety, budgets focused on classroom development, and an open-door policy on decision making. As Woods has been complicit if not a driving force behind many of the board’s controversies, we recommend Jenne Littrell.
### Board of Trustees Area 4: Steve Mull
Seat 4 is being vacated by Robert Shield, whose term ends this December and is not seeking reelection. Mull is the Grossmont Education Association’s pick for the seat. A former principal at Lakeside School District for 20 years, he has spoken out against the district’s library cuts and increases to the athletic budget. He’s also said that the board’s past actions of withholding support for LGBTQ staff and students is the wrong path for the district. Opposing Mull are Charissa McCullough, a registered Republican and Moms for Liberty activist who lists her occupations as mortgage loans and education, and Jimmy Moreno, an Independent. We trust the teacher’s union endorsement and positions Mull has taken as enough reason to recommend Steve Mull.
### Board of Trustees Area 5: Pete Mergens
Mergens is running against incumbent Jim Kelly. If you were keeping score in the rundown on the school board’s transgressions, he’s the guy who allegedly called a former teacher a “witch” and part of an “LGBTQ coven” in addition to recently defending the multimillion dollar athletic budget increase as helping “fatherless” kids from poor Black and Hispanic backgrounds.
